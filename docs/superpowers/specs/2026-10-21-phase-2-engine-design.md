# lazymkv phase 2: the engine design

- **Date:** 2026-10-21
- **Status:** draft for the owner's review

This document designs phase 2 of `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-04-lazymkv-design.md` ("the spec"), the row "2. Direct-mode engine" of spec 16. The spec stays binding: this document settles only what the spec leaves to phase 2, and part 13 lists every spec sentence that must change, once. P1 to P36 are the owner's answers of 2026-10-21 (part 2); they bind and are cited in parentheses, as in (P6). A bracketed id such as [ARCH-16] marks a builder call, a choice the designer settled without asking the owner. R-A1 to R-A12 are the designer's rulings made while assembling the drafted parts, and R-B1 to R-B4 those made after the builder check; they decide where the parts disagreed. K1 to K15 are the rulings on the review of pull request #5 (part 2.8), and L1 to L15 those on its second review (part 2.10). A choice marked "(designer's call; no builder call)" was settled here without a builder-call id. Part 14 lists the rulings and calls the owner should check while reading. Where texts disagree, an owner answer wins over a spec sentence not amended here, which wins over an R-A, R-B, K or L ruling, which wins over a builder call. Spec sections are cited as "spec 6.2 step 7"; this document's own subsections as "part 5.4". Earlier rulings keep their labels (D1 to D23, OR, RP, G, H, J).

## 1. Purpose, scope and acceptance

Spec 16 row 2 delivers `platform` for Windows, `tools`, `clients`, the engine with persistence and notifications, and `lazymkvctl`. Its acceptance is a known movie disc going from insertion to a filed file with no UI. The row also names five items for this design to settle:

| Item | Part | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| D17's bundling table | 11 | P13, P14, P16, P24 |
| The app's own recording format | 7 | P21 |
| The form of the tuning evidence | 7 | P21, P22 |
| The decoding of bitmap subtitles for OCR (D22) | 10 | P17 to P20, P26 |
| Code signing (RP10, OR11) | 11 | P14, P15, P28 |

### 1.1 What phase 2 delivers

- `platform` implements every trait of spec 4.3 except Autostart. The Windows implementations sit behind `cfg(windows)`, and in-memory fakes let whole fake jobs run on CI's three operating systems [ARCH-1].
- `tools` drives `makemkvcon`, `mkvmerge` and `ffprobe` as spec 4.2 lists them, and gains an `ffmpeg` driver for the decode of spec 6.4 step 10a [ARCH-1].
- `clients` covers TheDiscDB, TMDb, the Home Assistant webhook and the generic webhook.
- The engine performs the reducer's actions for the disc phase of spec 6.1, Direct mode of spec 6.2 and the file phase of spec 6.4, and sends the notifications of spec 12. Each job folder is a recording in phase 0's shape, with the job state and the decision record beside it (P21).
- `lazymkvctl` reaches the engine over the pipe and takes over `plan`, `decide` and `retune`. It reads a recording folder on a reachable path itself, and sends runs over the engine's jobs or the tuning evidence on the share to the engine (P23).
- Every title that reaches spec 6.4 step 10 is read by OCR, in every job (P3), with the app's own bitmap decoder and ocrs, within the bars of P17 to P20 and P26.
- CI builds a zip in the release layout (P24), with a minimal LGPL FFmpeg built in CI (P16). The release workflow, which attaches the tools' sources (P13) and has a signing step that stays empty for now (P14, P15), runs only as a dry run on a branch: its zip, sources and model files stay CI artifacts, and no tag is pushed and nothing is public until the owner says so (P28).

### 1.2 What the answers narrow

- **Unsure discs (P1).** A plan in whole-disc copy mode means title confidence is below `auto_threshold`. Such a job ends as a visible failure of class Unknown, keeps its plan and evidence, and its `failed` notification says that copy mode arrives in phase 3 (part 5.5). The disc stays in the drive, as spec 10.3 leaves a disc after any failure. Core does not change: the engine never performs the reducer's CopyDisc. Until phase 3, D1's "rip now and queue for review" holds only for discs whose title confidence reaches `auto_threshold`; a title-sure disc whose identity is unsure is still ripped and held at the disposition gate.
- **Held jobs and Discard (P2, P25, R-A1).** A job held for review sits in NeedsReview, its staging data untouched, until the owner approves it unchanged or phase 3's review commands exist. Phase 2's `lazymkvctl` has one review command, `approve <job>`, which accepts the shown reasons unchanged as core's `ReviewApproved` with no changes (P25). It has no discard for any job, held or failed. The pipe's `review_action` is implemented for `approve` with no changes only; every other review action and the `discard` request are refused with an error naming phase 3, so ENG-14's command list loses `discard`. A failed or cancelled job keeps its data until Retry or phase 3's Discard.
- **What that costs on the NAS (R-A1).** The staging data of failed, cancelled and unapproved held jobs stays on the share until phase 3: a raw rip of up to about 54 GB per UHD title. The retention cleanup never deletes it (spec 7). The owner may delete such a job's `<staging>\<job id>\` folder by hand, since staging is not a library root; a Retry of that job then fails.
- **OCR in every job (P3).** Step 10 runs on every title with two or more English or no-language subtitle streams in the file (`core::tracks::subtitle::step10_runs`), and every such title is read. The text and the frames go to the tuning evidence. Where the text's verdict differs, spec 8.7 adds `subtitle_ambiguous` with the note `subtitle_text_disagrees`, so the job is held until the owner approves it unchanged or phase 3 (P2, P25). Until experiment 14 answers, the text never drops a track and never sets or removes a flag (D20).
- **Retention by age only (P4).** The cleanup deletes the raw data of Filed jobs `retention_days` (default 7) after each was filed (part 5.9). Deletion oldest-first when space is short comes in phase 3, with AwaitingSpace's resolver.

### 1.3 Acceptance

1. **Experiment 9 first (P9).** The owner sets up the test library (P8), adds it to Jellyfin (P36), and runs phase 0's Task 16 in full before the phase 2 plan is drafted (part 12.5). A bad result reopens D11 and spec 8.9 before any planning. Run on 2026-10-21 (EXP-09-naming.md): the lone file and the two resolutions show as the spec expects; `combined` groups the cuts but shows the `{edition-...}` tag in the version name; the `jellyfin` layout shows them cleanly, so the default `edition_style` is `jellyfin` (spec 8.9 and 14, part 13).
2. **Two discs (P10).** The first is `pans-labyrinth-2006-uhd`: phase 1's plan of its recording gives identity 0.95 by fingerprint, title 0.98, Direct mode and no review reason, and its Spanish original makes the wanted languages English and Spanish (R4). Its decision holds the job with `commentary_detected`, for its cross-language commentary (D12, D15, RP7); the owner approves it unchanged with `lazymkvctl approve`, and it then remuxes, verifies and files (P25). The second is a movie disc that TheDiscDB does not know and TMDb identifies, at identity 0.90, which D9 lets file automatically. It tests the TMDb search route and the live answer shapes phase 1 only synthesised. It must carry two or more English subtitle streams, so that step 10 runs on a real disc; a `subtitle_ambiguous` hold is accepted and approved unchanged (P27). Ideally it carries Atmos or DTS:X, so that `ffprobe`'s profile string confirms or corrects OR7's provisional spellings and closes the first half of experiment 6.
3. **Where it files (P8).** The engine files into the test library's Movies folder and never writes to the real Movies and Shows shares. Staging and tuning go into subfolders of `lazymkv-staging` and `lazymkv-tuning` (part 6.2). A repeat run uses `lazymkvctl rip-again` with `movies_root` pointed at a fresh empty subfolder, so nothing is ever deleted inside a library root [MISS-DEV-19]. The engine on the mini PC uses the tools already installed there (P24).
4. **The pass check (P11).** A run passes when the engine's persisted plan and decision equal what `lazymkvctl plan` and `decide` give on the disc's recording, the `filed` notification arrives, and the owner confirms on Jellyfin (P36) that the file shows under the right film, plays, and has the expected default audio and subtitle tracks. Part 12.7 gives the comparison's exact procedure.
5. **Its place in the work (P12).** Acceptance comes after this design's pull request, the thin whole job on stand-in tools and the pull requests that widen it (part 12.6).

### 1.4 What waits for later phases

Phase 3 brings whole-disc copy mode, the review commands beyond an unchanged approval, with the rest of `review_action` (P25, R-A8), failure classes, the waiting-state machinery, the watchdogs, resume and the low-space half of retention. Phase 4 brings editions and TheDiscDB-mapped TV. Phase 5 brings the tray app, the autostart, the elevated UI with `ui_quit`, and the installer tool. A title that fails in phase 2 is still classed by the share check after its process exits (part 5.6); only the watchdog that ends a run when the share drops waits for phase 3. Part 14.1 lists every deferred item.

### 2.1 What phase 2 builds

- **P1 (Q-UNSURE).** What phase 2 does when the plan sends a disc to whole-disc copy mode. Answer: fail, and leave the disc in; the job is a visible failure with its evidence kept and a message that copy mode arrives in phase 3, and core is unchanged. It changes: the engine never performs CopyDisc in phase 2 (part 5.5); LIB-5's option of ejecting such a disc without ripping was not taken.
- **P2 (Q-HELD).** What the owner can do with a held job before phase 3's review commands exist. Answer: nothing until phase 3; held jobs sit untouched in staging, with no approve or discard in phase 2's `lazymkvctl`. It changes: R-A1 reads the answer literally, for failed jobs too (part 1.2); ENG-14's list loses `discard`. P25 later narrows it: a held job can be approved unchanged, and nothing else (part 2.7).
- **P3 (Q-OCR-SCOPE).** Whether the engine only measures subtitle reading or runs it in every job that needs it. Answer: run it in every job; every title reaching step 10 is read, its text and frames go to the tuning evidence, and the text may raise `subtitle_text_disagrees` reviews. It changes: the decoder, the line finding and the ocrs reading ship in phase 2, not as design only (OCR-1).
- **P4 (Q-RETENTION).** Whether the engine deletes the raw rips of filed jobs, as spec 7's cleanup does. Answer: by age only; raw rips of filed jobs are deleted after `retention_days`, and deletion when space is short is phase 3's. It changes: that half of spec 7's retention bullet moves to spec 16 row 3 (part 13).

### 2.2 Machines and shares

- **P5 (Q-START).** How the engine starts on the mini PC until phase 5's autostart exists. Answer: an on-demand desktop task; a script or `lazymkvctl` registers a scheduled task with no trigger that runs the engine elevated in the owner's desktop session, as phase 0's task did; `lazymkvctl` reaches it from SSH; quick stand-in runs may run in the SSH console with local folders. It changes: the engine runs in the one session that reaches the share and its saved credentials (part 3.1); registering the task is a standing change on the mini PC (part 15).
- **P6 (Q-PIPE-ACCESS).** Which of the owner's processes may talk to the elevated engine. Answer: elevated clients only; only elevated processes of the owner's account may connect, remote clients are refused, `lazymkvctl` runs elevated, and the engine starts the phase 5 UI elevated. It changes: it sets the pipe's DACL [ARCH-16] (part 3.4); with the protected data folder [MISS-ARCH-18], no non-elevated process can choose what the engine runs. The SSH account has administrator rights, since phase 0 ran MakeMKV over SSH, so `lazymkvctl` over SSH qualifies.
- **P7 (Q-SHARE-CRED).** Which credentials the engine uses when the logon session can already reach a share. Answer: session first, own fallback; the engine uses the session's existing access and connects with lazymkv's stored credential only on access denied. It changes: `ShareConnector`'s order (part 6.1); on the owner's machine no stored credential is needed.
- **P8 (Q-FOLDERS).** Which folders the engine writes during development and acceptance. Answer: a new test library folder beside staging, for example `lazymkv-test-library\Movies`, added to Jellyfin (P36), and the engine's staging and tuning as subfolders of `lazymkv-staging` and `lazymkv-tuning`. It changes: nothing is written to the real Movies and Shows libraries (part 6.2).

### 2.4 Shipping, signing and licences

- **P13 (Q-SOURCES).** How lazymkv meets the source duties of the GPL and LGPL tools it ships. Answer: attach sources to releases; each GitHub release carries the exact MKVToolNix and FFmpeg source archives and FFmpeg's build settings. It changes: the release job uploads them beside the zip (part 11.4).
- **P14 (Q-RESIGN).** Whether the bundled `ffmpeg.exe`, `ffprobe.exe` and `mkvmerge.exe` get lazymkv's signature. Answer: re-sign them in the release job, which Artifact Signing or an own certificate allows and SignPath's terms do not, since SignPath signs only a project's own binaries. Answer: unsigned until later; nothing is bought or applied for, the release job has an empty signing step, and the Microsoft Store route is rejected because Microsoft does not accept apps that need elevation for any part of their functionality. It changes: parts 11.2 and 11.3; the development engine keeps the installed FFmpeg (R-A2).

### 2.5 OCR

- **P17 (Q-OCR-BAR).** What the faster ocrs method must achieve before Tesseract is used. Answer: a time cap plus parity; the row-based method passes if a two-hour film's English track reads in at most 10 minutes (about 350 ms per event) and the rule's measures match the full ocrs method's on every phase 0 track; missing either brings in Tesseract; one trial on the phase 0 frames checks both. It changes: D22's fallback gets its bar (part 10.9). P26 supersedes "about 350 ms per event", which counted phase 0's rendered frames: the bar is 600 s for Pan's Labyrinth's English track of 887 events, about 676 ms per event (part 2.7).
- **P18 (Q-OCR-LINES).** What the app does when an event's lines cannot be split cleanly from its rows. Answer: irregular events (no clear gap, tall lines, many lines, text at top and bottom) go through ocrs's text detector, and both models are embedded, about 12 MB. It changes: the contested OCR-7 is settled; D22's two embedded models are right.
- **P19 (Q-OCR-FAIL).** What happens when reading a stream fails or runs too long. Answer: each stream gets a time cap of about three times its expected time; an overrun or an error discards the partial text and adds a new note, with no review. It changes: the note `subtitle_text_unread`, new in phase 2 and defined in part 10.8; the closed set of notes (`core::review::Note`) gains a fourteenth entry, written into spec 8.7.
- **P20 (Q-OCR-MODELS).** Where the ocrs model files come from when lazymkv is built. The owner first asked why a separate repository and a download at build were offered and what each means for users, and the question was asked again. Answer: upstream at build, on every release. `build.rs` downloads the text-detection and text-recognition models from ocrs's S3 bucket by pinned URL and SHA-256, an environment variable can point at a local copy (offline builds, the `box.sh` mirror), and the release job uploads the exact embedded bytes with the CC-BY-SA attribution as assets of every release; if the bucket moves, the URL is repointed at any release's copy. The owner rejected a dedicated assets release ("weird to use a release as a storage bucket"), a separate model-crate repository and committing the models to this repository. Precedents: Rust's `download-ci-llvm`, `ort` and `whisper.cpp` download at build; `webpki-roots`, `openssl-src` and `tessdata` keep data in separate repositories; `unicode-ident` carries a compound licence. It changes: D17 forbids only run-time downloads, so a release still never fetches anything, while a build from source needs the network or the local copy; D22's "small crates" clause is amended (part 13).

### 2.8 The review of pull request #5 (K1 to K15)

The review of 2026-10-21 left fifteen comments on this design and the spec. Each was checked against the code, the spec and the upstream sources, and all fifteen hold. Part 13 carries every spec sentence the rulings change, and part 14.3 lists K2, K3, K6 and K13 among the calls the owner should check.

- **K1 (zlib).** mkvmerge zlib-compresses PGS and VobSub tracks by default: Pan's Labyrinth's `evidence.mks` is 13,057,115 bytes, while its packets sum to 27,774,847 bytes. The minimal FFmpeg's `--disable-autodetect` leaves zlib out, so the release `ffprobe` would list compressed sizes, and part 10.3's cross-check would leave every bitmap stream unread. Ruling: the minimal build adds `--enable-zlib`, under zlib's own licence, and zlib's source archive joins the release's sources. The cross build compiles zlib from that pinned source archive rather than an apt package, so the build stays reproducible (part 11.3). Step 10's extraction keeps mkvmerge's default compression, so phase 0's evidence files and the engine's read alike, and the in-process reader keeps `miniz_oxide`. It changes: parts 4.7, 10.3, 11.2, 11.3, whose one-off check gains a step 10 listing, 11.4, 14.3 and 15.
- **K2 (OCR threads).** ocrs 0.13.1 and rten 0.26.0 run both models on rten's own global rayon pool, sized by `RTEN_NUM_THREADS` or else the physical core count, with no priority hook. The engine's three-thread below-normal pool of OCR-12 would therefore never govern the OCR work. Ruling: OCR runs in a worker process, `lazymkv-ocr.exe`, the `ocr` crate's binary. The engine starts it through `ProcessGuard` at below-normal priority with `RTEN_NUM_THREADS=3` in its environment, one run per stream, giving it the staged `evidence.mks` path, the stream id, `sub_clear_max_bytes` and the output folder. The worker writes `sub-<n>.ocr.jsonl`, the stream's totals in `sub-<n>.run.json` (L6) and EVID-5's crops, and exits. P19's per-stream time cap becomes a kill of the worker after the cap, with the partial output discarded. `lazymkv-dev ocr-trial` runs the same worker, so trial arm R measures what ships. Builder call OCR-12 is superseded. It changes: parts 3, 4.1 to 4.4, 4.7, where `rayon` leaves the table, 5.3, 9.4, 10.1 to 10.3, 10.7 to 10.9, 11.1, 11.5, whose layout gains `bin\lazymkv-ocr.exe`, and 14.3, and part 13's rows for spec 4.1's executables, 4.2's `ocr` row, 4.4's table and 6.4 step 10.
- **K3 (the slug).** Core's slug rule, `is_slug`, accepts lower-case letters, digits and hyphens, and a fingerprint is upper-case hex. A job folder named by its job ID would be skipped by `retune` and refused by `lazymkv-dev labels`. Ruling: job IDs are lower case. `core::job::job_id` lower-cases the fingerprint, a core change, so a job ID is `<fingerprint in lower case>-<n>` everywhere: paths, `lazymkvctl` and notifications. It is then a valid slug unchanged. The fingerprint itself stays upper-case hex in `meta.json` and the disc records, as phase 0 wrote it. It changes: parts 4.1 and 7.1, which says "lower case" once for parts 5.4, 7.7 and 9.5, 14.3 and 15, and spec 5's Job ID bullet (part 13).
- **K4 (a reproducible FFmpeg).** GNU ld stamps the link time into every PE image by default, so a rebuild never matches the SHA-256 the manifest records, and P28's dry run would fail at release step 3. Ruling: the build adds `-Wl,--no-insert-timestamp` to `--extra-ldflags` and sets `SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH` to the pinned source tarball's date, so two builds of the same inputs are byte-identical. The one-off check on the mini PC runs on the build workflow's artifact, and the manifest records that artifact's SHA-256s. A release, or P28's dry run, rebuilds and must reproduce them, and the release step fails otherwise. It changes: parts 11.2's configure line, 11.3 and 11.5's release step 3.
- **K5 (dev's decide).** `dev`'s `decide_doc` and `decision_doc` read every input from a `Recording` built by walking a folder, so no entry point renders the document from values, as R-B1 needs. ## 3. Processes, start-up and security on the mini PC

Phase 2 builds three of spec 4.1's four executables, `lazymkv-engine.exe`, `lazymkvctl.exe` and the OCR worker `lazymkv-ocr.exe`, which the engine starts for step 10's reading (part 10.7, K2); the tray application `lazymkv.exe` comes in phase 5. This part covers how the engine starts before phase 5's autostart exists, which processes may talk to it, how it protects what it runs while elevated, how it checks MakeMKV, and how it stops.

### 3.1 Starting the engine

The engine runs as a scheduled task in the owner's desktop session (P5). The task has no trigger; its principal is the owner's own account, logged on interactively, at the highest run level, which is spec 4.1's principal without the logon trigger. Phase 0's task `lazymkv-phase0` made every recording this way, because the shares, the saved credentials and the drive are reachable from the desktop session, and the share is denied over SSH.

A PowerShell script, `scripts/engine-task.ps1`, modelled on phase 0's `run-interactive.ps1`, registers and starts the task under the name `lazymkv-engine`. P5 allows a script or a `lazymkvctl` command; a script does it because Task Scheduler's own interface is COM, which phase 2 leaves to phase 5 [ARCH-3]. The script refuses to act while the task is running. Otherwise, run over SSH through `scripts/box.sh` like every other command on the mini PC, it works in this order:

1. It copies `lazymkv-engine.exe`, `lazymkv-ocr.exe` and `lazymkvctl.exe` from `C:\lazymkv\target\release` into a separate run folder, `C:\lazymkv\run`, so that the next build never meets a locked file and the next sync, which replaces `C:\lazymkv\src`, never removes what runs [DEV-16].
2. It sets the run folder's access list (part 3.5).
3. It registers the task with the run folder's copy of the engine as its action.
4. It starts the task and waits until `lazymkvctl status` answers.

The task starts only while the owner is logged on at the mini PC's own monitor, as phase 0's task did; a locked session is enough, and a logoff ends the engine.

Quick runs with the stand-in tools may instead run the engine in the foreground of the SSH console, with local folders under `C:\lazymkv\dev\` for staging, tuning and the library (P5, part 6.2). The engine's one optional argument, `--data DIR`, moves its data folder from `%ProgramData%\lazymkv` to `C:\lazymkv\dev\data` for such runs [DEV-16]. A console run has no share and no saved credential. Nobody knows yet whether a hidden window in an SSH session receives drive notifications, so a console run is never used with a disc.

### 3.2 Elevation

The engine runs elevated, because spec 4.1 requires it for MakeMKV. On Windows it checks its own token at start; without elevation it prints a message saying so and exits. The SSH session on the mini PC is elevated, since phase 0 ran `makemkvcon` and the eject there; the first platform pull request confirms this with the same token check.

### 3.3 One engine: the mutex and the pipe

Before anything else, the engine takes the named mutex `Global\lazymkv-engine` [ARCH-16]. The `Global\` namespace spans every logon session, so an engine started from the SSH console and one started by the task never run together. Any process may create a `Global\` mutex, so the engine creates it with a security descriptor limited to SYSTEM and Administrators. When the mutex already exists, the engine reads its owner: a mutex owned by the Administrators group was made by another elevated engine, and the engine says an engine is running and exits; any other owner is reported by name as a squatter, and the engine exits (designer's call; no builder call).

The pipe has a fixed name, `\\.\pipe\lazymkv` [ARCH-16]. The engine creates its first instance with `FILE_FLAG_FIRST_PIPE_INSTANCE`, so if another process holds the name, the engine fails to start and says why. Windows serialises the reads and writes of a synchronous pipe instance, so a writer would wait behind a pending read. The Windows `IpcTransport` therefore opens every instance with `FILE_FLAG_OVERLAPPED` and does its reads and writes as overlapped operations, each thread waiting on its own event (K12). `lazymkvctl` checks the other end too: before it sends anything, it asks Windows which process is the server and refuses one that does not run as the same user with an elevated token. A process that took the name while no engine ran therefore cannot block the engine unseen or pose as it (designer's call; no builder call).

### 3.4 Who may connect

Only elevated processes of the owner's account on the same machine may connect (P6). Three measures enforce this [ARCH-16]:

- The pipe is created with `PIPE_REJECT_REMOTE_CLIENTS`, so Windows refuses a connection over the network.
- The pipe's security descriptor grants read and write only to the owner's user SID and to SYSTEM, with a high mandatory integrity label that refuses reading and writing from a lower level. A non-elevated process of the owner cannot open it.
- After each connection, before sending the snapshot, the engine reads the client's token and drops a client whose user differs from its own or whose token is not elevated.

`lazymkvctl` therefore runs elevated, from an SSH session or an elevated terminal at the desktop. Spec 4.3 left open whether a non-elevated process of the owner may connect; P6 answers no. Because every client is elevated, `settings_set` may change any key, the tool paths included: a client that could set a tool path could already run that program elevated itself.

### 3.5 The data folder, the run folder and engine.log

The elevated engine reads from `settings.json` which programs to run, and keeps jobs, sessions and remembered decisions beside it. If a non-elevated process could write there, it could choose what the engine runs. The engine therefore protects the folder [MISS-ARCH-18]:

- It creates `%ProgramData%\lazymkv` when the folder is absent; a folder created by an elevated administrator is owned by the Administrators group.
- If the folder exists and its owner is neither Administrators nor SYSTEM, the engine refuses to start: another process made it and may have planted files.
- At every start it sets a protected access list that everything beneath inherits: full control for SYSTEM and Administrators, read for the owner's account, nothing for anyone else.

The owner's account keeps read access, so `lazymkv-dev` and a non-elevated terminal can read job records, which hold no secrets. The `--data` folder of a development run gets the same owner check and access list. The script gives `C:\lazymkv\run` the same access list, because the elevated task runs the program inside it, and a folder created under `C:\` otherwise lets every authenticated user write; this extends MISS-ARCH-18 from the data folder to the program the task runs (designer's call; no builder call).

The engine writes its own log, `engine.log`, in the data folder; it is new in phase 2. It holds what belongs to no job: starts, stops, refused clients, arrivals that made no job (part 5.4) and undelivered notifications, which spec 12 says are logged. In a console run the same lines also go to standard error.

### 3.6 Finding the tools

Spec 4.4's lookup order stands: the path in settings, then the release's `resources` folder, then the default install folder or registry entry, then `PATH`. Phase 2 adds one rule [MISS-ARCH-18]: a folder on `PATH`, or a program file in it, that a non-elevated process of the user could write is skipped, and so is a `resources` folder that such a process could write (part 11.5). The test uses the user's filtered token, the token a non-elevated process of the user gets, so it answers exactly what such a process could do. A tool path in settings is taken as given, because only an elevated client can set it (P6) and only an administrator can write `settings.json`; it stays the way to name any tool, wherever it sits. The Program Files install folders are protected by Windows' own access lists.

On the mini PC, phase 2 uses the tools already installed there (P24). `makemkvcon64.exe` and `mkvmerge.exe` are found in their default install folders, `C:\Program Files (x86)\MakeMKV` and `C:\Program Files\MKVToolNix`. `ffprobe` and `ffmpeg` came from winget into the owner's profile and reach `PATH` only through the WinGet `Links` folder, which the owner can write, so the new rule skips them; `settings.json` names them as `tool_paths.ffprobe` and `tool_paths.ffmpeg`. A non-elevated process of the owner could still replace those winget files. That risk is accepted on the development machine only; the release layout's `resources` folder removes it.

### 3.7 The MakeMKV check

The engine runs spec 6.1 step 1's call, `makemkvcon64 -r --cache=1 info disc:9999`, at three points [ARCH-9]: at engine start; after each insertion, as the drive listing before a job's first `makemkvcon` call, which part 5.4 runs after the fingerprint and the job lookup (R-A3); and at each self-test. From the output it reads the version in `MSG:1005` and any licence or key message spec 9.4 names: 5021, 5050, 5052, 5055 or 5095. Phase 0 never saw those messages, so their parsing is tested on stand-in output written by hand. The result goes into the snapshot's self-test results, with the program's path and how it was found. A self-test that comes while a `makemkvcon` runs reports the last check's result, marked busy, and starts no second `makemkvcon` (part 5.3).

A failed check never refuses a disc. ARCH-9 proposed refusing it with a notification until phase 3, but phase 1's reducer already has AwaitingTool and AwaitingMakeMkv, so the job waits there (R-A3) and the owner sees the disc left in the drive with a `waiting` notification naming the reason:

- **No `makemkvcon64`, or a version below `makemkv_min_version`:** the engine feeds ToolMissing for makemkvcon, and the job waits in AwaitingTool. A later self-test that finds a usable `makemkvcon64` delivers ToolFound, once the disc-phase token is free (part 5.4, way (d)).
- **A licence or key message:** the job goes on to the scan, which prints the same message and fails with class Licence, so the job waits in AwaitingMakeMkv; the notification carries the exact message (spec 10.2). VersionMessage is never fed in phase 2, because NeedsMakeMkvUpdate's message is not captured.
- **A changed version or key state:** when a check finds a version or key state different from the one recorded, the engine delivers MakeMkvChanged to every job in AwaitingMakeMkv, each once the disc-phase token is free (part 5.4, way (d)). A job waiting here holds no token, so another disc's drive listing can run this check (L2).

### 3.8 Stopping the engine

A new request, `shutdown`, stops the engine, and `lazymkvctl stop` sends it [ARCH-15]. While any job has a step running, the engine refuses it and names those jobs; waiting, held, failed and filed jobs do not count. `lazymkvctl stop --force` sends it with `force` set, and the engine then:

1. cancels each running step as `cancel` does (part 9.4); a CheckSpace or Eject in flight, which `cancel` refuses, is let finish first (designer's call; no builder call);
2. deletes each such job's partial final file in its library root's `.lazymkv-partial` folder;
3. persists every job and exits.

The rips and the rest of the staging data stay for Retry or phase 3's Discard (R-A1). In a console run, Ctrl+C is a forced shutdown. On exit the engine releases the mutex and closes the pipe, and the task ends. A logoff, or ending the task from Task Scheduler, stops the engine without the request; ProcessGuard's Job Objects then end its tools, and at the next start the engine performs what `core::job::resume` returns for each job [PIPE-1-interim].

### 3.9 What of spec 4.1 waits for phase 5

- The logon trigger, and the `Autostart` implementation that creates and removes that task (spec 4.3).
- `lazymkv.exe`, which the engine starts elevated (P6) and restarts when it exits unexpectedly, with the `ui_quit` message (R-A8).
- D22's commands on the UI program; until then they are `lazymkvctl licenses` and `lazymkvctl export-models` [ARCH-17] (part 9.6).

### 4.4 Threads and channels

The engine uses OS threads and `std::sync::mpsc` channels, with no async runtime [ARCH-4/ENG-6/DEV-10]:

- **The job-owner thread** holds the state of every job. Everything reaches it as a message on one channel. For each event it runs `core`'s reducer, persists the new state before any action runs (part 7.3), hands the actions to workers and broadcasts `job_update`. It never runs a tool, waits for the network or writes to a client itself.
- **Workers.** Each action runs on its own thread and reports back as an event. At most one disc-phase step and `file_phase_concurrency` file-phase steps run at a time (spec 6).
- **Tool runs.** Each running tool has one reader thread per output stream. Lines pass through `core`'s line reader [PIPE-10] and come back as progress and as the step's final event.
- **The drive thread** runs the `DriveWatcher` window's message loop.
- **Lookups.** One lookup thread per job runs spec 8.1's chain, one request at a time (part 8.2).
- **The notifier thread** delivers notifications with three attempts and backoff [ENG-21].
- **The timer thread** serves `StartTimer` actions.
- **The pipe.** One thread accepts connections; each client gets a reader and a writer thread, which do overlapped reads and writes on the client's pipe instance, each waiting on its own event, so the writer never waits behind the reader's pending read (part 3.3, K12). The writer drains a bounded channel, and the engine disconnects a client that falls behind rather than slow down.
- **OCR.** Step 10's reading runs in the worker process `lazymkv-ocr.exe`, which the engine starts under ProcessGuard at below-normal priority, one run per stream; a worker thread waits for it as for any tool run (part 10.7, K2).
- **Engine-side commands.** `plan`, `decide` and `retune` over the engine's jobs run as a child `lazymkvctl` under ProcessGuard (part 9.5).

A handful of long-lived activities around a synchronous, pure reducer fit threads without a runtime. Phase 1's runner is already blocking `std::process`, and tokio with reqwest would bring the largest dependency tree in the workspace into every J4125 build.

### 5.3 Rules every tool run follows

- **One `makemkvcon` at a time.** Before each start the engine checks, with `platform`'s process snapshot, that no earlier `makemkvcon` is alive and that the MakeMKV GUI is not running (spec 10.4) [PIPE-3]. If either check fails, the step fails as `Unknown`, and the message names the process found.
- **Flags fixed per job.** A job keeps the settings it started with [ENG-19], `minlength_s`, `cache_mb` and the job profile among them, so a disc's flags never change mid-job (spec 9.2).
- **Debug logging.** While `keep_tuning_evidence` is on, every `makemkvcon` call adds `--debug`, and part 7.2 keeps MakeMKV's log with the registration key removed [PIPE-9]. No setting is added.
- **Reading `makemkvcon` output.** A pure line reducer in `core` reads the output one line at a time [PIPE-10]. It is told which call it reads: the drive listing; a scan; or a rip, with the planned title's index and either its source file or, on a DVD, its DVD title number. It reports the version (`MSG:1005`); a rejected profile (`1009`); Java in use (`3344`); the titles added (`3307`, `3308` or `3028`) and the index guard's verdict at the saving message (`5015`, else `5014`); progress (`PRGV`); read errors (`2003`) and write errors (`2019`); the licence messages of spec 9.4; forced-track removals (`4047`); each title's result (`5003`, `5004` and `5037`, `5005` and `5036`); and scan completion (`5011` followed by a `TCOUNT` line). `tools` only feeds lines, kills and lists folders. The reducer is tested on the recordings on CI's three systems. The whole-text parser `core::robot::parse` still reads the finished scan for the planner.
- **Phase 0's conventions** [PIPE-14]. `MSG:5010` on the drive listing is ignored, because that call normally ends with it. After any kill the engine waits 10 s before the next `makemkvcon` call. `mkvmerge -J` passes on exit 0, or on exit 1 with its warnings logged. Tool output is decoded as UTF-8 with invalid bytes replaced. Phase 0's probe script also accepted exit 1 from the subtitle extraction and the remux; the engine does the same, and the verification of the output decides.
- **Hard limits** [PIPE-3]. Every external process runs under `ProcessGuard`, with these limits and no others: header-only calls (`mkvmerge -J`, `ffprobe -show_streams`) get 120 s; a scan longer than `scan_timeout_min` (30) is killed and retried once; after a `makemkvcon` run prints its final message, the engine waits up to `exit_wait_min` (30) for it to exit, then kills the process tree and does not eject; and the OCR worker's run for one stream is killed at P19's time cap (part 10.8). A limit that is hit fails the step as `Unknown`, with the full log and the exit code; for the exit wait, the message says that the process did not exit. The OCR worker's cap is the one limit that does not fail the step: it leaves the stream unread and gives P19's note `subtitle_text_unread` (part 10.8). Phase 3 brings the rest of spec 10.4: the silent-scan rule, the `PRGV` stall and the read-error rate, the `mkvmerge` stall, ending a run when the share drops, and turning "did not exit" into `MediaReadError`. Until then a rip that hangs before its final message is bounded only by `cancel` [ENG-13].
- **Priority.** `mkvmerge`, `ffprobe` and `ffmpeg` run at below-normal priority. Spec 6 names only the first two, because step 10a's `ffmpeg` pass came later with D16. The OCR worker `lazymkv-ocr.exe` runs at below-normal priority too (part 10.7, K2).
- **Recording.** Every run is recorded capture-style (part 7.2) [EVID-10], so each job folder keeps phase 0's recording shape (P21).

### 5.4 The disc phase: spec 6.1, steps 1 to 5

**Arrival (R-A3).** `DriveWatcher` reports an arrival, debounced per drive, with the drive's id and volume root. The engine waits `drive_settle_s` (10), then up to `drive_ready_timeout_s` (120) for the volume to mount, as spec 6.1 step 1 says. At engine start, and after a `settings_set` that leaves no problem (part 9.4), the engine treats every disc already in a drive as an arrival, except the disc of the job that takes the token at a restart (part 5.10) and, at a restart or after a `settings_set`, a disc whose drive record in `drives.json` says its arrival was already handled (L1a, part 5.10). Such a disc is fingerprinted and matched to its job at once, then routed or queued exactly as a fresh insertion would be (L1). The arrival queue lives in memory and is never persisted; it is rebuilt from the drives. Then, at once and for every arrival:

1. It lists the volume read-only and computes the fingerprint and, on a Blu-ray or UHD, the disc ID from `AACS/Unit_Key_RO.inf`; a DVD is looked up by its fingerprint alone [LIB-6]. It reads the volume label and the `bdmt` disc name. No `makemkvcon` runs for this, so the one-`makemkvcon` rule is untouched, and no arrival's fingerprint or job lookup waits for another disc's phase (R-A4).
2. A fingerprint that cannot be computed, before any job exists, is logged in `engine.log`; the disc stays in, and a `failed` notification with class `Unknown` and no job ID goes out.
3. `core::job::job_lookup` over the earlier jobs of that fingerprint gives one of three answers. `AlreadyRipped` sends `already_ripped` and ejects at once, outside the reducer, so a held job's disc inserted again gets `already_ripped`. `NewJob` and `Resume` need the disc-phase token below: while another job holds it, the arrival is queued before any job is created or event fed (K8). Otherwise, and when the queue later takes the arrival, `Resume` routes the disc to that job: a job in a disc-phase state that waits for its disc, or one that failed with `MediaReadError` in its disc phase, gets `MediaArrived` and takes the token; a job in a waiting state that returns to the disc phase only has its disc noted as present and takes no token, because the reducer takes `MediaArrived` in no waiting state and the engine performs a disc-phase action only with the job's disc in a drive. `NewJob` creates the job with `job::job_id` and `job::new_job`; the `Fingerprint` action that `new_job` returns is already done, so the engine copies the navigation files into the job folder, writes the recording's files (part 7.1) and feeds `FingerprintDone`, whose `RunScan` follows once the job holds the token.

**The disc-phase token [PIPE-12] (R-A4, K8, L2).** One disc is in its disc phase at a time, and a token in the engine's memory says which. The token is held only while a disc-phase step of its job may run. A job takes it in one of four ways: (a) as a new job created from an arrival; (b) as the job an arrival is routed to with `MediaArrived` (step 3); (c) as the job chosen at a restart, the oldest job in a disc-phase state whose disc is in a drive (part 5.10); (d) by a Retry, or a return from a waiting state, that re-enters a disc-phase state (part 9.4). Each of (a), (b) and (d) queues while another job holds the token, and takes it when the token is free. A job releases the token when its disc phase ends: when it reaches `Ejected`, after an eject or after `eject_failed`, or when it fails. It also releases the token when it enters a waiting state, AwaitingMakeMkv, AwaitingTool, AwaitingNetwork, AwaitingSpace, AwaitingShare or AwaitingLookup, and when its disc is removed and the reducer answers `MediaRemoved` with `WaitForDisc`. Its disc stays in the tray through a wait (spec 7). The job takes the token back only through (b) or (d), queueing like an arrival for its disc's `MediaArrived`, a return or a Retry. Whatever waits for the token, an arrival, a Retry or a return, waits in one queue, in the order it came. When the token is free, the engine takes the next in the queue: a new job is created and fed `FingerprintDone`, a disc is routed to its job as step 3 says, or a Retry or return is delivered. A Retry or return delivered while the job's disc is in no drive runs no disc-phase action: the engine feeds `MediaRemoved`, the job waits for its disc, and the token is free again. Since no waiting job holds the token, a new disc in the same drive goes ahead while a pulled disc's job waits, a waiting job's own disc reaches it when the token is next free, and the queue cannot deadlock. For the same reason, another disc's drive listing can run ARCH-9's check while a job waits in AwaitingMakeMkv, and end that wait (part 3.7, spec 10.1). A queued disc that is removed leaves the queue.

**Step 1, the drive listing and the MakeMKV check.** Before each `makemkvcon` call the engine runs `makemkvcon64 -r --cache=1 info disc:9999` and reads the `DRV` lines; the drive index is the line whose seventh field equals the drive id `DriveWatcher` reported, the drive letter on Windows and a scenario token in the fakes. The index is never cached. The first such call of a job after an insertion is ARCH-9's check (part 3.7), so a failed check sends the job from `Fingerprinted`, or the resumed state, to AwaitingTool, or through the scan to AwaitingMakeMkv. The listing is saved as `drive-list.txt` (part 7.1).

**Step 2, the recording.** The engine writes `meta.json` and `files.json` and copies the navigation files into the job folder (part 7.1). The plan later applies any remembered decisions of the fingerprint.

**Step 3, the database lookups.** `RunScan` starts TheDiscDB and TMDb beside the scan; the disc phase never waits for them [ENG-8] (part 8.2).

**Step 4, the scan.** At job start the engine reads `appdata.tar` from the folder of the `makemkvcon64` that the lookup found, and `core`'s ustar reader extracts `default.mmcp.xml` (designer's call; no builder call). It writes the job profile into the job folder as `job.mmcp.xml` with `core::profile::make_job_profile`, a name unique to the job and the selection `+sel:all,-sel:mvcvideo,=100:all` [PIPE-11]. MakeMKV's file is never shipped (D17). Whole fake jobs use a fixture `appdata.tar`, built at test time from a hand-made profile, beside the stand-in. The scan is:

`makemkvcon64 -r --messages=-stdout --progress=-same [--debug] --minlength=<minlength_s> --cache=<cache_mb> --profile=<job profile> info disc:N`

A scan succeeds only with `MSG:5011` followed by a `TCOUNT` line, whatever the exit code (spec 9.2, D18); without them it fails as `Unknown`. A licence message during the scan is fed as `ScanFailed` with class `Licence`, which leads to AwaitingMakeMkv (part 5.10). The scan records the version and whether `3344` appeared. ARCH-7 ships no Java runtime; when the tool lookup finds no Java, the engine feeds `ToolMissing { tool: java }`, which only records `label_rule_unavailable`. On a `MSG:1009` during the scan, the engine writes a fallback profile into the job folder as `fallback.mmcp.xml`: MakeMKV's own default profile from `appdata.tar` with every `defaultSelection` attribute replaced by `+sel:all,-sel:mvcvideo`, through the same `make_job_profile` (K6, L4). Without that replacement the default profile would select by the user's own `$app_DefaultSelectionString`, which nothing in `core` evaluates. The engine runs the scan once more with `--profile=<fallback profile>`, and every later call of the job names it too, so the scan whose numbers the plan uses and every rip carry identical flags (spec 9.2) [PIPE-11]. There is one fallback only: a `MSG:1009` on that second scan fails the job with `ScanFailed`, class `Unknown`, and a message quoting MakeMKV's rejection, and nothing is ripped on MakeMKV's own default selection (L5). The fallback leaves out a 3D title's MVC video, as the job profile and `Selection::All` do, but the job profile's `=100:all` does not apply after the switch, so the rip keeps every other stream in MakeMKV's default order. Verification (part 5.6 step 7) checks the stream set with `Selection::All` and applies spec 6.2 step 7's order rule as for any rip, so a rip that MakeMKV orders otherwise fails verification; this is what spec 9.3 means when it lets verification decide whether the result is usable. Core is unchanged, and the job record says the fallback profile was used. A `1009` first seen on a rip fails that title as `Unknown`.

**Step 5, identify and plan.** `core::plan::plan` runs over the scan, the answers in hand and the remembered decisions, and the engine persists the plan with its evidence. A Direct plan is fed as `PlanDone { mode: direct }`, and `Planned` asks for `CheckSpace` (part 5.8); a Copy plan follows part 5.5. The titles are ripped, probed, remuxed, verified and renamed in the order of `Plan.titles`, which fixes the `NN` of each title key: movies in feature order, which is the database's index order for spec 8.3 rule 1, then labelled cuts, then group order. The index guard resolves each title's index again at every call, so plan order never depends on MakeMKV's numbering. A TV disc keeps the core's behaviour: `tv_root` stays unset, so a mapped TV disc is held with no path [LIB-9], and an unmapped one is a Copy plan.

### 5.5 Plans that need whole-disc copy mode (P1)

A plan whose title confidence is below `auto_threshold` chooses Copy mode (spec 6.3), and phase 2 does not copy the disc. The engine persists the plan and its evidence, then feeds `PlanFailed` with class `Unknown` from `Scanned`; the job becomes `Failed { Unknown, from: Scanned }`, and the reducer sends `failed`. The job record and the notification's message say that the disc needs whole-disc copy mode, which arrives in phase 3; this is message text, not one of core's notes, so core is unchanged. The disc stays in the drive (spec 10.3), the disc phase has ended, and a queued disc goes on. Nothing was ripped, so the job holds no staging data; its job folder keeps the plan and the evidence. Retry plans again, after the refetch of part 8.4, and fails the same way until phase 3. Inserting the disc again starts a new job beside the failed one, because `job_lookup` treats a disc-phase `Unknown` failure that way.

### 5.6 Direct mode: spec 6.2, steps 6 to 8

**Step 6, rip.** For each planned title in plan order, the engine empties `<staging>\<job id>\rip\<title key>\` and runs:

`makemkvcon64 -r --messages=-stdout --progress=-same [--debug] --minlength=<minlength_s> --cache=<cache_mb> --profile=<job profile> mkv disc:N <index> <staging>\<job id>\rip\<title key>\`

The line reducer runs spec 6.2 step 6's index guard before the saving message. On a mismatch the engine kills the process tree, waits 10 s, scans again with the job's flags, saved as `rescan-<n>.txt`, maps the planned title to its new index by source file, segment map and duration, and retries as that step says; a third mismatch fails the title as `Unknown`.

A title succeeded when a success summary reports one title, none of `5003`, `5004` or `5037` appeared, and the folder holds exactly one `.mkv` (spec 9.4); the engine then feeds `TitleRipped`. A failed title is classed with `core::job::classify_title_failure`, using only what is known when the process exits: `ShareConnector`'s reachability and free space for staging, and whether `2019` and `2003` appeared. A licence message during a rip is fed as `LicenceMessage`. Every other failure is `Unknown`, including `DriveIncapable`, `NeedsNetwork` and `NeedsMakeMkvUpdate`, whose messages are not yet captured (spec 10.2); this is the reading of PIPE-1-interim and PIPE-3. A removed disc kills the running call, the engine feeds `MediaRemoved`, and the killed call's result is dropped (R-A7); reinsertion routes `MediaArrived` to the job (part 5.4), which rips the title again.

**Step 7, verify.** The engine runs `mkvmerge -J` on the title's one file under the 120 s limit, then `core::verify::verify_rip` with the selection `all`, which the job profile and, after a `1009`, the fallback profile both give, each leaving out MVC video as `Selection::All` does (part 5.4, K6, L4). A first mismatch is fed as `TitleVerifyFailed { second: false }`, and the reducer re-rips the title; a second goes on, and the engine records `verify_failed` from `verify::failure_reviews` for the decision.

**Step 8, eject and notify.** After the last title verifies, the reducer is in `RipVerified` and asks for `Eject` [PIPE-16]. The engine first closes every handle it holds on the volume: the listing and fingerprint reads, the navigation copies and `DriveWatcher`'s handle notification. It then runs the four steps of phase 0's eject, lock, dismount, allow removal and eject, all four even when an earlier one fails, and reports `EjectFailed` only when the eject step fails; EXP-07 found all four succeed on both drives. The engine never feeds the media removal its own eject causes. `Ejected` sends `disc_done`; `EjectFailed` ends the disc phase the same way and sends `eject_failed`.

### 5.7 The file phase: spec 6.4, steps 9 to 15

**The slot.** One job holds the file-phase slot at a time (`file_phase_concurrency` 1), first in, first out, and the file phase of earlier discs runs while the next disc rips (spec 6). Before granting the slot, the engine runs part 8.3's extra lookup round for a job whose plan is lookup-pending; it feeds `LookupPending` there, in `Ejected`, and otherwise only as the outcome of a first Decide in `Inspecting` that cannot fetch a stale TMDb copy again (part 8.4), never while a step of the job runs. It grants the slot by feeding `FilePhaseSlot` to the oldest job in `Ejected { after_copy: false }`. The job holds the slot until it reaches `Filed`, `NeedsReview`, `Failed` or `Discarded`, including while it waits in a state entered after the slot was granted (designer's call; no builder call). Retry of a job that failed in its file phase, and an approval (part 5.10), are delivered only when the slot is free for the job, as `core::job`'s `Retry` and `ReviewApproved` events require of the engine; until then the request is accepted and queued first in, first out with the jobs in `Ejected`, and the job then holds the slot.

**Step 9, inspect.** For each staged title the engine runs `ffprobe -v error -show_streams -show_format -of json <raw>` under the 120 s limit, the call phase 0 recorded as `ffprobe-streams.json`.

**Step 10, subtitle evidence.** The engine asks `core::tracks::subtitle::step10_runs`, the one copy of the rule: step 10 runs when two or more English or no-language subtitle streams remain after the `4047` removals. For each such title it runs `mkvmerge --gui-mode -o <staging>\<job id>\evidence\<title key>.mks -D -A -s <ids> <raw>`, then, for each subtitle stream of the small file, `ffprobe -v error -select_streams s:<n> -show_entries packet=pts_time,duration_time,size -of json <evidence file>`. The English and no-language bitmap streams then go to OCR (P3, part 10), whose text reaches spec 8.7's text rule.

**Step 10a, packet MD5 and tuning evidence.** While `keep_tuning_evidence` is on, one `ffmpeg` pass over each title's audio uses EXP-13's fixed graph. It gives the content rule's measures, the packet MD5 of every audio stream through the `streamhash` muxer, and the compact copy as FLAC in Matroska (part 7.6). While the setting is off, one pass reads the audio packets without decoding: `ffmpeg -i <raw> -map 0:a -c copy -f streamhash -hash md5 <file>`. Both passes use the FFmpeg that the tool lookup finds: a release ships P16's minimal build in `resources\` (part 11), and the engine on the mini PC uses the installed FFmpeg 9.0.2 (P24, R-A2). The pass writes into the staged tuning folder `<staging>\<job id>\tuning\<title key>\`, never straight to `tuning_root`. While the setting is on it writes the level folder, with its `streamhash.txt`, and the compact copy; while it is off, only a `streamhash.txt`. The job's recording gets a second copy of the level folder or of that `streamhash.txt` (part 7.1). While the setting is on, a separate step copies the staged evidence to `tuning_root` (part 7.5) [EVID-4].

**Step 11, decide.**

1. The engine first fetches again any stale TMDb copy it reads (part 8.4 defines stale and says what the engine feeds when it cannot). When the plan is still lookup-pending, it then runs `plan::refresh_identity` from the saved answers [ENG-9] (part 8.4).
2. It reads the library for `LibraryFacts` and collisions at the exact paths the namer builds [LIB-7] (part 6.4).
3. For each title it builds the decision inputs in memory, as `dev`'s `DecideInputs` value (K5), from what it scanned, probed and measured: the scan, the probes, the verification report, the packet MD5s, the measures, the subtitle events, the text and the unread streams, and the job's settings. Only after a restart does it read them back, from the job folder and the staged tuning folder (part 5.10). It then calls `decision::decide` (R-B1).
4. It calls `decision::same_path_reviews`, renders each title's `decide.json` with `dev`'s pure renderer `decide_doc` (part 7.1, R-B1, K5) and feeds `job::decision_done`.

**Step 12, the disposition gate.** The reducer holds a job with any review reason, any low confidence or a missing path in `NeedsReview { held: disposition_gate }` and sends `needs_review`. Phase 2 offers only the owner's `approve`, which accepts the shown reasons unchanged (P2, P25, part 5.10).

**Step 13, remux.** After the space check, the engine runs one `mkvmerge` per title, in plan order:

`mkvmerge --gui-mode -o <library root>\.lazymkv-partial\<job id>-<title key>.mkv.partial <track options> <raw>`

The track options come from `core::tracks::layout`, as spec 8.10 requires: `--audio-tracks`, `--subtitle-tracks`, `--track-order`, and per track `--language`, `--track-name`, `--default-track-flag`, `--forced-display-flag`, `--hearing-impaired-flag` and `--original-flag`. Chapters are kept. `RemuxDone` is fed once every title is written.

**Step 14, verify and file.** For each partial file the engine runs `mkvmerge -J` under the 120 s limit and `core::verify::verify_final`; every file must match its decision and the source duration within 2 s before `FileVerified` is fed. `File` then renames each title in plan order without replacing anything (part 6.5) [LIB-8], and the job records each filed file's path, size and modification time. A file that appeared at the final path since the decision makes the rename fail, and the engine feeds `FileFailed` with class `Unknown` (part 6.5).

**Step 15, notify.** `Filed` sends `filed`. The raw rip stays for `retention_days` as an undo (part 5.9).

### 5.8 Space checks

`CheckSpace` uses `core::plan::staging_bytes_needed` before a Direct rip and `core::plan::write_bytes_needed` before a remux, with free space from `ShareConnector`. When staging and a library root are on one volume, by part 6.1's rule, each check adds two amounts [PIPE-7]: this job's later steps on that volume, so the rip check counts its own remux; and the space already promised to other jobs' unfinished steps, such as an earlier disc's remux beside this rip. The promises are kept in memory, released as each step finishes and rebuilt from the persisted states at engine start, so a passed check means the job can finish. For the owner, staging and libraries are folders of one share with tens of terabytes free, so the sums change nothing there. A short check feeds `SpaceShort`, which leads to AwaitingSpace; an unreachable root feeds `ShareLost` (part 5.2).

### 5.9 Retention (P4)

A cleanup task deletes `<staging>\<job id>\`, the staging tuning folder included, of `Filed` jobs whose `filed_unix` (part 7.3) is older than `retention_days` (7). It runs at engine start and then hourly (designer's call; no builder call). In the same pass it deletes, in every job folder under `%ProgramData%\lazymkv\jobs\`, each TMDb copy, `tmdb.json`, `tmdb-record.json` or `tmdb-search.json`, whose `fetched_at` is more than six months old, whatever the job's state (part 8.4, P29, R-B4). It skips the staging data of a job whose tuning evidence is still pending (spec 7). Apart from that deletion, it never deletes anything under `%ProgramData%\lazymkv\jobs\`. It never touches `tuning_root`, where part 7.5's refresh mirrors the deletion, or phase 0's folders, and never deletes the staging data of held, failed or running jobs. Deletion when space is short arrives with AwaitingSpace's resolver in phase 3.

### 5.10 Waiting states, restarts and held jobs

**Waiting states [PIPE-1-interim].** The engine enters every waiting state the reducer gives and leaves it only through what spec 10.1 names or a `lazymkvctl retry`, which makes the engine check the condition again and, if it has cleared, feed `ShareBack`, `SpaceFreed`, `MakeMkvChanged` or `ToolFound`, as fits the state. AwaitingNetwork returns on its 10-minute timer; phase 2 never enters it, because `NetworkNeeded`'s message is not captured. AwaitingLookup follows part 8.3. AwaitingMakeMkv also returns when ARCH-9's check, at engine start, at an insertion or at a self-test, finds the version or key changed (part 3.7); the insertion's check is another disc's drive listing. AwaitingTool also returns when a self-test finds the tool. No other poll or resolver is built. During the disc phase the disc stays in the tray for as long as the wait lasts (spec 7), but the job releases the disc-phase token while it waits, and a return to a disc-phase state queues to take it back (part 5.4, L2).

**Restarts (L1).** At start the engine feeds `EngineRestart { disc_present }` to every job that is not `Filed`, `Discarded`, held or failed, then performs what `core::job::resume` returns [PIPE-1-interim]. The disc-phase token and the arrival queue live in memory, so a restart starts with the token free and the queue empty (K8). Of the jobs in a disc-phase state whose disc is in a drive, the oldest takes the token (part 5.4, way (c)) and is fed `disc_present` true. Every other job in a disc-phase state is fed `disc_present` false and waits for its disc. A waiting job holds no token (L2), and its `disc_present` says whether a drive holds a disc with its fingerprint. The engine then treats every other disc already in a drive as an arrival (part 5.4): it is fingerprinted and matched to its job at once, then routed or queued exactly as a fresh insertion would be. A disc that sat in the queue before the restart, the disc of a job that waits for it, and the reinserted disc of a job that failed with `MediaReadError` are therefore taken up again without being pulled and reinserted. `resume` empties a rip's title folder and rips again, deletes a remux's partial file and runs it again, runs a header-only step again, makes a disc-phase job without its disc wait for it, and recreates the timers of AwaitingNetwork and AwaitingLookup. One exception keeps a restart or a `settings_set` from repeating a finished arrival (L1a, designer's call): `drives.json` records, per drive, the fingerprint of the disc last seen in it and whether that arrival was handled, true once the disc was routed to a job, taken from the queue, found already ripped or ejected, and false while it waits in the queue. A disc that matches its drive's record with handled true only has its presence noted; every other disc in a drive is an arrival. So a disc left in after a copy-mode failure (P1) or a failed eject starts nothing at a restart, while a disc that sat in the queue does.

**Held jobs.** A job in `NeedsReview` stays as it is, untouched in staging, until the owner approves it with `lazymkvctl approve` or phase 3's review commands exist (P2, P25). An approval accepts the shown reasons unchanged; the reducer then runs Decide again over the staged evidence, and the job goes on to remux, verify and file (part 9.4). That Decide uses the inputs the engine kept in memory for the held job. After a restart, as for any Decide alone in `Decided`, the memory is gone, so the engine reads back, with `dev`'s readers, the same `DecideInputs` as `lazymkvctl decide` (K5), from the job folder and the staged tuning folder rather than `tuning_root`. The recording comes from the job folder, and the packet MD5s and the OCR text from the staged tuning folder `<staging>\<job id>\tuning\<title key>\`, laid out as part 7.1 says; for a job run with the setting off, the MD5s come from that folder's bare `streamhash.txt`, a reading `dev`'s readers gain in phase 2, and the measures are absent, as they are for any such job. The `--text` layout of part 7.7 is only the command line's way of naming such a folder. `job.log` records the read-back (designer's call; no builder call). Before either Decide, the engine fetches again any stale TMDb copy it reads, and feeds `DecisionFailed` with class `Unknown` and the reason when it cannot (part 8.4). The job is held again if the decision made again raises a reason beyond those approved, or if the job lacks a final path or has `path_collision`, which only phase 3's commands can clear. The engine refuses `discard` and every other review action (R-A1), and retention never deletes a held job's staging data; the cleanup's six-month deletion of TMDb copies applies to every job (part 5.9, L11). ENG-2 recommended approve-as-is with discard, and ENG-14 left the held-job commands to the owner; P25 takes ENG-2's approve-as-is without its discard.

### 5.11 mkvmerge progress and its one-off check

The remux and the subtitle extraction run without `-q` and with `--gui-mode` [PIPE-2]. Their `#GUI#progress` lines feed the IPC `progress` message, and their `#GUI#warning` and `#GUI#error` lines go to the run's log (D16). In phase 2 these lines drive no timeout, because the `mkvmerge` stall rule is phase 3's.

The spec's stall rule is contested. EXP-01 saw an empty remux log until `mkvmerge` exited, but that run used `-q`, which suppresses progress. Before the driver relies on these lines, one check runs on the mini PC. It installs nothing and writes only under `lazymkv-staging`. A small script, run through phase 0's `run-interactive.ps1` in the owner's desktop session, runs `mkvmerge --gui-mode -o <file> -D -A -s <ids> <raw>` under `lazymkv-capture record` on the kept `pans-labyrinth-2006-uhd/all-tracks` rip. That extraction read the whole 54 GB file for 764 s in phase 0 and wrote 13.1 MB, a long run with a small output, and the run's `.timing.jsonl` shows whether the progress lines arrive spread over the run or only at exit. If they arrive only at exit, the stall rule goes back to the owner before phase 3, and phase 2 shows no remux progress.

## 6. Shares, credentials and folders

This part covers how the engine reaches the network roots, which folders phase 2 writes, how it reads the library before filing, and how it files without overwriting. Spec 4.3, 5 and 7 bind; the rulings are P7 and P8.

### 6.1 Reaching a share: the session first, lazymkv's credential second

`ShareConnector` connects only for a root given as a UNC path: `staging_root`, `movies_root`, `tv_root` or `tuning_root`. A local root never connects, but `ShareConnector` still reports its reachability and free space.

The engine first uses the access its own logon session already has (P7). It runs elevated in the owner's desktop session (P5), as phase 0's task did, and that task reached `\\unas-pro\media` through the owner's credential saved in Windows; on the owner's machine this first attempt is expected to succeed, so lazymkv's own credential can stay unset in phase 2. The engine uses its own credential only when the first attempt is refused with `ERROR_ACCESS_DENIED` or `ERROR_LOGON_FAILURE`, which is how Windows reports that the session holds no credential the server accepts; any other error means the share is unreachable. The fallback reads the credential for that `\\server\share` from `SecretStore`, connects deviceless with `WNetAddConnection2W`, with no local name and without `CONNECT_UPDATE_PROFILE`, and tries the access once more. The share counts as unreachable if no credential is stored or Windows refuses the connection, and the error says which. Windows refuses a second credential for a server the session already reaches under another (`ERROR_SESSION_CREDENTIAL_CONFLICT`); phase 0 never checked this, so the engine reports that error by name and does not retry.

lazymkv's credential is a generic Credential Manager entry, one for each `\\server\share`, with the target name `lazymkv:share:\\server\share`. Windows never sends a generic credential to a server by itself, so storing one changes nothing the owner's session already does. The owner sets it with `lazymkvctl secret set share:\\server\share`, giving the user name and the password as two lines on standard input (part 9.4) (designer's call; no builder call). The engine writes it to the vault from the desktop session, because a key-based SSH logon cannot reach the owner's Credential Manager, and after that the secret never crosses the pipe again (spec 4.5).

Free space comes from `GetDiskFreeSpaceExW` on the root. ### 6.4 Reading the library: LibraryFacts and collisions

### 6.5 Filing with a rename that never replaces

Filing (spec 6.4 step 14) first creates any missing folders of the final path, then moves the partial file with `MoveFileExW`, without `MOVEFILE_REPLACE_EXISTING` and without `MOVEFILE_COPY_ALLOWED` [LIB-8]. In a test library only lazymkv writes to, no phase 2 run should meet this race, and phase 3's review data model gives it the hold LIB-8 intends. Part 14 lists this as a contested call.

A folder can also be present to the share and closed to the engine (L7). In experiment 9, folders deleted from another machine were reported by the share as already existing for some minutes, while no listing showed them and nothing could be written into them (EXP-09-naming.md). A `retry` an hour or more later tries again, since the state cleared on its own within minutes in experiment 9. No listing of the library root is needed, so LIB-7 stands: the test is the failed open of the folder the share said exists. Phase 3's `path_collision` review takes the case over (part 14.1). Part 15 item 1 has the owner empty the test library in a way that avoids the state.

## 7. Job records, persistence and the tuning evidence

### 7.1 Each job folder is a recording (P21)

The job folder `%ProgramData%\lazymkv\jobs\<job id>\` is a recording in phase 0's layout (spec 15 "Recordings"), with the job ID as its slug and each title key as a rip label, and the job's state and decision records beside the recorded files. A job ID is lower case, `<fingerprint in lower case>-<n>`, because `core::job::job_id` lower-cases the fingerprint (K3). ### 7.2 Every external run, recorded capture-style

The `tools` crate records every run of `makemkvcon`, `mkvmerge`, `ffprobe` and `ffmpeg` the way `lazymkv-capture record` does [EVID-10]: standard output to `<name>`, standard error to `<name>.stderr`, the time each output line arrived to `<name>.timing.jsonl`, and the exit code to `<name>.exit.json`. The stand-in tools replay exactly these files (spec 15). Step 10a's `ffmpeg` pass is the exception: it keeps phase 0's names inside the level folder.

While `keep_tuning_evidence` is on, every `makemkvcon` call gets `--debug`, and no separate setting controls it [PIPE-9]. MakeMKV 2.0.0 writes its debug log anew on every run to `%USERPROFILE%\MakeMKV_log.txt`, in the profile of the account it runs as, the owner's (P5), and ignores a path given to `--debug`. After each call exits, the engine saves that file as `<name>.makemkv-debug.txt` beside the run's output, provided it was written after the call started. Before saving, it replaces every occurrence of MakeMKV's registration key with `[app_Key removed]`, as phase 0 did; the key is read from `app_Key` under `HKCU\Software\MakeMKV`, held in memory only for that replacement, and never logged. Only one `makemkvcon` runs at a time, so the file always belongs to the call just ended. A log that is missing, older than the call, or cannot be copied never fails the job; `job.log` says which. `--debug` adds `MSG:1003` and `MSG:1004` lines, which spec 9.4 already ignores.

### 7.3 Job state: state.json and transitions.jsonl

A job's state lives in two files [EVID-7]. `state.json` is rewritten atomically on every transition, with the format `lazymkv job v1`. It holds the job ID, the fingerprint, the drive and the creation time; the `core::job::State`, with its return state or failure; whether rip again is armed (`job_lookup`'s third input); the plan, and each title's progress: attempts, verification and read errors; each title's `FileDecision`; `filed_unix`, the time the job entered `Filed`, which retention reads (part 5.9); each filed file's exact path, size and modification time; the recorded collisions [ENG-22]; and the job's notes, such as `tuning_evidence_pending`, with the state of its tuning copy. Following RP23, lists are sorted by name and are never maps keyed by a number.

Each transition is handled in this order: `core::job::reduce` gives the new state and the actions; `state.json` is replaced; one line holding the time, the event, the old and new states and the actions is appended to `transitions.jsonl` and flushed; then the actions run. This keeps spec 10.1's rule that every transition is persisted before its actions run, and a crash between the second and third steps loses at most one log line, never a state. `transitions.jsonl` is never replayed: after a restart the engine reads `state.json` and performs what `core::job::resume` returns [PIPE-1-interim], so a later change to the reducer never changes how an old job reads back. The log serves history and diagnosis only.

### 7.4 File format and write discipline

`state.json`, the remembered-decision files and `drives.json` are versioned serde JSON [EVID-11], each with a `format` string: `lazymkv job v1`, `lazymkv remembered v1` or `lazymkv drives v1`. The engine refuses a file whose format is missing or names another major version, reports it and never overwrites it; season sessions get the same treatment in phase 4. Every write goes to a temporary file in the same folder, flushed with `FlushFileBuffers`, then put in place with `MoveFileExW` and `MOVEFILE_REPLACE_EXISTING | MOVEFILE_WRITE_THROUGH`; a temporary file a crash left behind is removed at the next write of the same file.

Three kinds of file keep the shape they have. Settings files, `%ProgramData%\lazymkv\settings.json` and each job's copy, keep phase 1's shape with no `format` key, because `Settings` refuses an unknown key. Phase 0-shaped files keep phase 0's shapes, and those that had a format string keep it, such as `audio-proxy.json` with `lazymkv audio proxy v1`. Remembered decisions are the core's `Remembered` type plus the `format` key, which `plan --remembered` ignores because the type does not refuse unknown keys; phase 2 reads one when present, and writing waits for phase 3's Remember (P2).

`drives.json` records each `DriveIncapable` failure as one entry: the drive's name as MakeMKV prints it in its `DRV` line, without the serial number (spec 3); the disc's format as `meta.json` gives it; the job ID and the time; and MakeMKV's message. Phase 2 fixes this format, but it never produces `DriveIncapable`, whose message is not captured (part 5.6), so the writer is tested by a unit test only. The warning that reads the file belongs to the phase 5 UI (spec 10.2). It also holds, under `drives`, one record per drive keyed by `DriveWatcher`'s drive id, with `fingerprint` (the disc last seen in it, or null when empty) and `handled` (true once that disc's arrival was routed to a job, taken from the queue, found already ripped or ejected; false while it waits in the queue) (L1a, part 5.10). Only this flag is stored: the queue itself is rebuilt from the drives at start. The record is rewritten at each arrival, removal and queue change, with the same write discipline.

Evidence is staged before it is copied [EVID-4]. Step 10a's pass and the OCR write into the staged tuning folder `<staging>\<job id>\tuning\<title key>\` of part 7.1, which has the dataset folder's shape, the OCR reading under its `subs-ocr\` (R-A6). While the setting is on, the level folder is written into the job folder and the staged folder; while it is off, only `streamhash.txt` is (part 7.1 says what the staged tuning folder holds and that it exists in every job that reaches step 10a). A separate step then copies each file to `tuning_root` as `<name>.partial`, checks its size and renames it. An unreachable `tuning_root` therefore never stops the decision: the job goes on with `tuning_evidence_pending` (spec 7), and a retry is only a copy, never a second decode. The staging copy counts as the job's raw data, which retention keeps until the copy succeeds. The dataset files are copied first, and the level folder's `audio-proxy.json` is written last, in the job folder and in the copy, as in phase 0, so a level folder holding `audio-proxy.json` still means the proxy is in the dataset.

The engine refreshes `<tuning_root>\recordings\<job id>\` each time the job stops in `Filed`, `NeedsReview` or `Failed`, and after any later change to the job folder. A refresh copies only changed files, replaces only its own earlier copies and never writes the owner's file names (part 7.7). It mirrors the cleanup's deletion of a TMDb copy older than six months (part 5.9, R-B4), the one case where it removes a file; a TMDb copy in `<tuning_root>\recordings` is otherwise the same as the job folder's. Pending copies are retried at engine start and whenever `ShareConnector` finds `tuning_root` reachable before another step. With `keep_tuning_evidence` off nothing goes to `tuning_root`; the OCR text in staging still serves the decision and is deleted with the raw data.

### 7.6 The compact audio copy and its headroom

While `keep_tuning_evidence` is on, each title gets one `audio-proxy.mka` in phase 0's form, the copy spec 7 describes, made in step 10a's single `ffmpeg` pass. `audio-proxy.json` keeps phase 0's format `lazymkv audio proxy v1` and its keys, with the job ID as `slug` and the title key as `label`.

The copy is stored 18 dB below the measured signal, recorded as `gain_db` -18. Phase 0 treated a track that peaked within 0.1 dB of full scale as possibly clipped and failed the run. The engine instead makes the title's proxy again from the staged file at 30 dB below, recorded as `gain_db` -30 [EVID-8], which `levels-from-proxy` already reads. This costs one more full read of the title, 20 to 40 minutes for a UHD title on the J4125. The decision's own measures are taken in float, so the redo does not touch them. If the proxy still clips at 30 dB, the title's evidence fails as spec 7 says: the dataset gets no proxy for that title, and `job.log` names the stream.

### 7.7 retune reads both places (P22)

`retune` takes several evidence folders and reports on each separately. In phase 2 there are two: the phase 0 clone, `corpus/owner` or its mirror on the mini PC, and `<tuning_root>\recordings`. `--evidence` may be given more than once, each as `DIR` or `DIR=TEXTDIR`, where `TEXTDIR` is that folder's OCR text in the layout `<text>\<slug>\<label>\subs-ocr\sub-<n>.ocr.jsonl`; the split is at the first `=`, and a single `--evidence` with `--text` keeps today's meaning (designer's call; no builder call). The phase 0 clone pairs with phase 0's dataset in `lazymkv-tuning`, and `<tuning_root>\recordings` with `<tuning_root>\dataset`, whose layout matches with the job ID as slug and the title key as label. Retune reads the records' `pts` and `lines` unchanged, and it can re-read the kept crops or `evidence.mks` with another OCR engine without the disc. It reads `original_language` from each recording's `tmdb.json`, and fetches a stale copy again first, as every reader does (part 8.4, R-B4).

Spec 15's requirement to reproduce `corpus/owner/audio-dataset.json` applies to the phase 0 clone only. Each folder has its own optional `audio-fitted.json` and baseline `audio-dataset.json`, and the engine's folder starts with neither. The engine's folder holds its level series, unlike the evidence clone, so `retune` over it never ends for lack of level files. The owner's labels for an engine job are `audio-labels.json` and `subtitle-labels.json` in `<tuning_root>\recordings\<job id>\`, which keeps each label with its track's evidence (D16); the engine's refresh never writes those names.

A `retune` over the share goes through the engine (P23, part 9.5). A label edit under `<tuning_root>\recordings` runs `lazymkv-dev labels` in the desktop session, at the mini PC's own console; phase 2's `command` carries only `plan`, `decide` and `retune`. No engine job recording or OCR text is committed to `lazymkv-evidence` (P22); only the dated live lookup records, by the owner's hand (part 8.5, R-B3). A pull script comes later.

## 8. Lookups and notifications

The `clients` crate holds the four clients spec 4.2 names. Its only job is to move bytes: the phase 1 parsers in `core::lookup` parse every answer, so a change in a service's answer shape breaks one parser in one place. The crate depends on `core` alone and builds and tests on all three CI systems [ARCH-1].

### 8.1 Transport

The clients use `ureq`, a blocking HTTP client, with rustls on the ring provider and the roots of `webpki-roots` compiled in, and with default features off except TLS [ENG-7/DEV-11]. The stack builds with the MSVC tools already on the mini PC, behaves the same on Linux and macOS, and fits the engine's threads, which run without an async runtime [ARCH-4]. aws-lc-rs, rustls's default provider, needs a C build with CMake; CMake is installed on the mini PC, so ring is chosen to keep the build light, not to work around a missing tool. Because the roots are compiled in, TLS trust does not follow Windows' root updates, and each release updates them; it also means the clients refuse an https webhook with a self-signed certificate, while a Home Assistant on the LAN that serves plain http is reached over http.

Every request times out after 10 seconds, matching spec 12's webhook timeout [ENG-7]. Each job runs its lookups one at a time on one lookup thread of its own. A job makes only a handful of requests, so there is no cache shared between jobs; the answers saved in the job's folder are the only cache. Every request carries a `User-Agent` naming lazymkv and its version (designer's call; no builder call). The clients reach the network through a small transport trait; tests swap in a transport that replays recorded answers keyed by URL, which gives spec 15's "recorded HTTP responses" with no server or network.

The TMDb credential is the owner's API read access token, sent as `Authorization: Bearer` on TMDb's v3 endpoints, as phase 0 sent it [ENG-7]. It is kept in `SecretStore` as `tmdb_token` and never crosses the pipe once set (spec 4.5). TheDiscDB needs no credential. The self-test checks the token with `GET /3/authentication`, which passes on HTTP 200, the call phase 0 used to confirm the token (designer's call; no builder call).

### 8.2 What is asked, and in which order

A job's lookup thread starts with the `RunScan` action, which follows the fingerprint and the job lookup once the job holds the disc-phase token (part 5.4, K8), so the lookups start with the scan and run while it runs (spec 6.1 step 3) [ENG-8]. The thread follows spec 8.1's chain and asks only for what the chain needs next:

1. TheDiscDB by fingerprint at `/api/dischash/{hash}`, then by disc ID at `/api/discid/{id}`. A DVD has no disc ID yet and is asked by fingerprint only; libdvdread's DVD disc ID stays deferred until the owner has a DVD that TheDiscDB knows by its ID [LIB-6].
2. The disc name's search comes straight after TheDiscDB's two requests, without waiting to learn whether TheDiscDB knows the disc [ENG-8]. A generic name is never searched (spec 8.1). The request is `/search/multi`, first page only, with the cleaned name from `core::lookup::name`. When the name's `tagged` form still differs from its `cleaned` form once both are folded, a second search runs with `tagged`, and the two pages are merged as J8 says: the cleaned name's results first, then the tagged name's results not already among them, compared by kind and ID. The merge is a pure function in `clients`, tested on recorded pages (designer's call; no builder call).
3. When a TheDiscDB record carries a TMDb ID, the client fetches `/movie/{id}` or `/tv/{id}` with `append_to_response=external_ids`. When the entry has no TMDb ID, `/search/multi` runs with the entry's title, and `core` prefers the entry's kind and year among the results (spec 8.1).
4. When no TheDiscDB record gives an identity and spec 8.1's plausibility rule leaves exactly one search result, the client fetches that result's record as in item 3.
5. For a TV record, the client fetches the season answer for the parsed season and merges it with `tmdb::merge_season`; when no season was parsed, it fetches every season the record lists, because spec 8.1's TV runtime check then uses the whole series.

When TheDiscDB supplies the identity, the speculative name search's answer is saved but not used.

### 8.3 When a lookup is pending

Answers are classed by HTTP outcome [ENG-8]. An HTTP 200 whose body the `core` parser accepts is answered. An HTTP 404 is an answered miss: TheDiscDB's miss is a 404 with an empty body (spec 8.1), and TMDb's 404 on a record means it has no such record. A timeout, a refused connection, an HTTP 5xx, a 429 or a 401 leaves the lookup pending; a 401's waiting message says that TMDb refused the token. With no token set no request is made, and the waiting message says so. A 200 whose body does not parse is pending too, with the parser's sentence in the job's log and the waiting message, so a corrected service or parser ends the wait without losing data (designer's call; no builder call).

Planning never waits (spec 8.1). At the end of the scan, spec 6.1 step 5 plans with the answers in hand, marks any unanswered lookup pending in the plan (`identity.lookup_pending`), and its requests carry on [ENG-8]. When a job with a lookup-pending plan reaches `Ejected`, and before it gets the file-phase slot (part 5.7), the engine runs one more round at once. A round asks again for every unanswered lookup, waits for a request still in flight rather than sending it again, and restarts spec 8.1's chain at its first unanswered step, so a late TheDiscDB answer can still lead to its TMDb record within the round. Only a lookup still pending after this round makes the engine feed `LookupPending` in `Ejected`; part 8.4 gives the one other case, in `Inspecting`. `LookupPending` moves the job to AwaitingLookup, starts its 60-minute timer and sends `waiting` with the reason `lookup`. A merely slow answer therefore costs seconds, not an hour.

In AwaitingLookup, each expiry of the timer runs a round. When it leaves nothing pending, the engine feeds `LookupAnswered`; otherwise it feeds `LookupPending`, and the reducer restarts the timer. `lazymkvctl retry` on a job in AwaitingLookup runs a round at once [PIPE-1-interim]. After a refused token, the owner sets a new one with `lazymkvctl secret set tmdb_token` and retries. The engine feeds `LookupAnswered` only when every lookup the plan marked pending has an answer; when only some answer, those are saved and the job keeps waiting.

### 8.4 Answers saved, and the identity refreshed before Decide

Every answer is written into the job's recording when it arrives, whatever state the job is in, through a temporary file and a rename [ENG-9, EVID-11]. The recording keeps phase 0's `thediscdb-hash.json`, `thediscdb-discid.json`, `tmdb.json` in phase 0's slim form and `lookup-status.txt`. Beside them are the merged search page and the TMDb record the plan used, which `plan` reads through `--tmdb-search` and `--tmdb-record` (part 7.1). Both are slim copies with `fetched_at`, part 7.1 giving each file's fields; no TMDb answer is ever kept whole (P29).

Before every Decide, while the persisted plan is lookup-pending, the engine builds `LookupInputs` from the saved answers, runs `plan::refresh_identity`, persists the refreshed plan and decides with it [ENG-9]. The reducer's `RefreshIdentity` action runs the same code. The refresh is pure, keeps the ripped titles, and returns the plan unchanged when no new answer has arrived. This closes OR13's two lookup cases without changing `core`:

- **An answer that arrives outside AwaitingLookup**, such as a request still in flight when the plan is made that finishes during the rip. The reducer accepts `LookupAnswered` only in AwaitingLookup, so the engine feeds nothing, and the saved answer reaches the decision at the next Decide.
- **A restart between an answer and the refresh.** `resume` gives `Ejected` no action and `Inspecting` only `Probe`, so no `RefreshIdentity` follows the restart; the refresh before Decide runs from the saved answers instead.

Each saved TMDb copy is slim, carries the day it was fetched (`fetched_at`) and is never a full answer (P29): `tmdb.json` in phase 0's shape, `tmdb-record.json` in that shape plus the fields the plan uses that phase 0's file lacks, and `tmdb-search.json` in `/search/multi`'s shape (part 7.1). There is no trimmed shape, so `core::lookup::parse_recorded` and `dev plan`'s TMDb reader are unchanged. The rule is phase 0's: a copy is stale when its `fetched_at` is more than six months old, or when the cleanup below has deleted it; whenever a stale copy is read, the reader first fetches it again, a record by its TMDb id from the plan in `state.json` and a search page with the same queries. It slims the answer the same way, replaces the file and goes on (TMDb's API Terms of Use 1.C). The engine fetches with the token in `SecretStore`. A local run of the `dev` library, `lazymkv-dev` or `lazymkvctl` over a folder it is given, fetches with `TMDB_TOKEN` from the environment when it is set, as phase 0's scripts did. Otherwise it exits 3 with a message naming the stale copy, the code spec 15 and `retune` already give to evidence that could not be read or checked (K15). `retune` reads `original_language` from `tmdb.json` and follows the same rule.

Nothing is fetched on a schedule. The cleanup task of part 5.9, at engine start and then hourly, deletes each TMDb copy in a job folder whose `fetched_at` is more than six months old, whatever the job's state, so no TMDb copy older than six months sits in a job folder. The plan in `state.json` keeps the TMDb id, title, year and original language as the job's own facts (`core::lookup`), so a deleted copy is fetched again by that id, or a search page with the same queries, when it is next needed. Part 7.5's refresh mirrors the deletion into `<tuning_root>\recordings\<job id>\`. The phase 0 clone's copies are the owner's, as today.

When the engine cannot fetch, because no token is set, TMDb refuses the token or no answer comes, the outcome depends on the read. The first Decide, which runs in `Inspecting`, feeds `LookupPending`, and the job waits in `AwaitingLookup` as part 8.3 says, the stale copy counting as a pending lookup. A plan in `Scanned` after a Retry, a restart or a return from a waiting state feeds `PlanFailed` with class `Unknown` and the reason. A Decide in `Decided` after an approval, a Retry, a restart or a return from a waiting state feeds `DecisionFailed` with class `Unknown` and the reason. In the last two cases `retry` tries again. A `plan --job`, `decide --job` or `retune --jobs` run the engine performs for `lazymkvctl` (P23) exits 3 with the reason and leaves the job's state alone (K15). The reducer gains no row, since it takes `LookupPending` in `Ejected` and `Inspecting` to enter `AwaitingLookup`, and in `AwaitingLookup` to restart the timer. This is R-B4, a designer's call. Until the UI exists, `lazymkvctl licenses` prints TMDb's attribution (part 9.6) [ARCH-17].

### 8.5 Live shape checks

The TMDb answers phase 1 tested against are synthetic and follow TMDb's documented shapes. Phase 2 checks them against the live services with `#[ignore]` tests in `clients` [ENG-20]. They run only on the mini PC, with `cargo test -p lazymkv-clients -- --ignored`, and read the token from the `TMDB_TOKEN` environment variable the owner set there on 2026-10-05. CI never contacts either service, and no token is ever stored at GitHub. The tests make a fixed list of public requests:

- TheDiscDB's `dischash` and `discid` for a fingerprint and a disc ID taken from `corpus/thediscdb-api`;
- `/search/multi` for one name whose `tagged` and `cleaned` forms differ, so both J8 searches and their merge run;
- `/movie/{id}` and `/tv/{id}` with `external_ids`, and one `/tv/{id}/season/{n}`, for TMDb IDs that TheDiscDB's public answers already give.

Each answer goes through the parser the engine uses. A test fails when a parser refuses an answer, or when a field `core` reads is missing from an answer that should have it. TheDiscDB's answers are then saved as received. Each TMDb answer is saved in two forms (P29): the slim record of the fields `core` reads, with `fetched_at`, and a shape record that holds only the answer's field names and JSON types, no values. The tests write these dated records into the evidence mirror folder on the mini PC that `LAZYMKV_EVIDENCE` names, never into this repository; the mirror has no `.git`. Nothing is committed automatically (P22): the owner copies the records into the clone on the Mac and commits them by hand (R-A9, R-B3). The `core` and `clients` tests replay the saved records when the clone is present and skip otherwise, as the golden tests do. A saved TMDb record more than six months old is fetched again by a new run of the live tests, as the clone's README says (P29).

### 8.6 Notifications

Both webhook URLs are secrets [ENG-17]: a Home Assistant webhook URL contains the webhook ID, the only thing guarding the owner's automation. They are kept in `SecretStore` as `ha_webhook_url` and `webhook_url` and set, like `tmdb_token`, with `lazymkvctl secret set <name>`, which reads the value from standard input. The snapshot reports only whether each URL is set and its host. Both http and https URLs are accepted.

One notifier thread sends the notifications in the order they are raised and keeps them in memory only [ENG-21]. Each channel is delivered on its own, with the same JSON body as a POST, and a delivery counts on any 2xx. Each attempt has spec 12's 10-second timeout; there are three attempts, the second 1 second after the first fails and the third 4 seconds after the second. A notification that still fails is logged in `engine.log` and dropped; it never blocks a job, as spec 12 accepts. A restart loses a notification still being retried, and the eject and the job record remain the primary signals. A channel whose URL is not set is skipped. The self-test's webhook check (spec 13) opens a connection to each URL's host without posting, so it never fires the owner's automation (designer's call; no builder call).

The payload has spec 12's fields. The event-specific fields come from `core::job::NotifyEvent` as it serialises: `reason` for `waiting`, `reasons`, a list, for `needs_review`, and `class` for `failed`. A `failed` sent for an arrival that never became a job (part 5.4) has a null `job_id`.

Phase 2 adds no event; the notifier sends every `Notify` action the reducer emits, and all eight of spec 12's events can arise: `disc_done` after a disc phase and its eject; `already_ripped` when a disc past its disc phase is inserted again without rip again [MISS-DEV-19]; `filed` at spec 6.4 step 15; `needs_review` for a held job, which then waits for the owner's `approve` or phase 3 (P2, P25); `waiting` and `resumed` for any waiting state the reducer enters [PIPE-1-interim], with `lookup` the reason phase 2 exercises on purpose; `failed` for every failure, including a copy-mode plan (P1, part 5.5), a cancel [ENG-13] and a hard process limit [PIPE-3]; and `eject_failed` when the `Ejector` reports a failure.

## 9. IPC and lazymkvctl

### 9.1 Transport and envelope

Spec 4.5's protocol stands: newline-delimited JSON over the pipe of part 3.3, every message shaped `{ "v": 1, "type": ..., "id": ..., "payload": ... }`. Phase 2 settles the rest [ARCH-16, ENG-10]. `v` must be 1; any other `v` gets an error reply with the message's `id`, and the connection stays open. An unknown `type` gets an error reply, and unknown fields inside a payload are ignored, so a newer client's extra field does not break an older engine. A client line longer than 4 MiB gets an error reply, and the engine disconnects that client. Replies carry the request's `id`; pushes carry `null`, except `command_output`, which carries the `id` of its `command`.

### 9.2 Clients, the snapshot and history

Any number of clients may connect at once, and each gets every push [ENG-10]: one `lazymkvctl` can watch over SSH while another sends a command, and in phase 5 the UI and `lazymkvctl` connect together. On connect, after part 3.4's token check, the engine sends `snapshot`: a summary of every job not finished plus the 50 most recently finished (filed, failed or discarded); the settings without secrets, with the problems `Settings::validate` reports; for each secret whether it is set, and for a webhook URL only its host [ENG-17]; and the self-test results, with part 3.7's MakeMKV check and each tool's path, version and where it was found. A client that reconnects gets a fresh snapshot (spec 4.5). `history`, new in phase 2, asks for finished jobs older than a given job and gets a page of at most 50 summaries. `job_update` carries state changes with the job's plan and review reasons (spec 4.5) and, for a job held with `path_collision`, the colliding paths, which the job record keeps until phase 3's Resolve collision [ENG-22] (OR13). `progress` arrives at most once a second per job.

### 9.3 Messages and their payloads

The field names below are the interface; the plan's second pull request fixes them with the serde types in `core` (part 12.6) (designer's call; no builder call).

### 9.4 Requests in phase 2

| Request | Source | Phase 2 |
|---|---|---|

**`settings_set`.** The payload names only the keys it changes [ENG-19]. The engine applies it to the current settings and reads the result through `Settings::from_json`. It refuses the whole request if the result is unreadable, has an unknown key or has any problem, listing them in the error, and nothing changes; otherwise it writes `settings.json` atomically and replies with the new settings. Each job keeps the settings it started with, copied into its job folder when it is created, so a disc's MakeMKV flags never change mid-job (spec 6.1 step 4) and the copy is spec 7's record of the thresholds in force. `settings.json` can still have a problem: the defaults have one until `tuning_root` is set, and a hand edit can add one. While it has a problem, or while `staging_root` is unset, which `Settings::validate` does not report because only the engine requires it, the engine starts no job (designer's call; no builder call). A disc that arrives then stays in the drive, `engine.log` says why, and the snapshot shows the problem. After a `settings_set` that leaves no problem, the engine treats every disc already in a drive as an arrival, by the one rule of part 5.4 (L1), except a disc whose drive record in `drives.json` says its arrival was already handled (L1a, part 5.10).

**`secret_set`.** The payload names a secret and gives its value; the engine stores it through `SecretStore` and replies without echoing it, and the value never leaves the engine again (spec 4.5). The names are `tmdb_token`, `ha_webhook_url`, `webhook_url` [ENG-17] and `share:\\server\share` for a share credential, whose value is the user name and the password on two lines (P7, part 6.1). Any other name is refused.

**`retry`.** On a failed job, `retry` delivers Retry, which the reducer answers by resuming at once, so the engine holds it until the job may run its step (L3). A Retry that re-enters a disc-phase state is delivered when the disc-phase token is free (part 5.4, way (d)); a job that failed in its file phase gets it only when the file-phase slot is free for it (part 5.7). On a job in a waiting state, `retry` runs again the check the state waits on [PIPE-1-interim] (part 5.10); a return it causes to a disc-phase state waits for the token in the same way.

**`cancel`.** `cancel` is accepted only while a step of the job is running [ENG-13]. The engine kills the running tool's process tree, the OCR worker's included (part 10.7), and feeds the step's failure with class `Unknown` and the message "cancelled by the owner", which the job record keeps with the log. A step that runs no tool is let finish, and the engine feeds the failure in place of its result. The killed worker's own result is dropped, and only the engine's event is fed (R-A7). The job ends in `Failed`, the disc is not ejected (spec 10.3), a `failed` notification goes out as for any failure, and the data stays until Retry or phase 3's Discard (R-A1). No reducer event is added, so `core` does not change. A `cancel` for a job with no running step is refused, and the reply names the job's state.

|---|---|
| `Fingerprint` | `FingerprintFailed`, class `Unknown` |
| `RunScan` | `ScanFailed`, class `Unknown` |
| `Plan` | `PlanFailed`, class `Unknown` |
| `CheckSpace` | refused |

| `Decide` in `Decided` | `DecisionFailed`, class `Unknown` |
| `Remux` | `RemuxFailed`, class `Unknown` |
| `VerifyFile` in `Remuxing` | `FileVerifyFailed`, class `Unknown` |
| `File` | `FileFailed`, class `Unknown` |

The same dropping rule covers the kill on a removed disc, where the engine feeds `MediaRemoved` instead (part 5.6).

**`discard`.** Refused with an error naming phase 3, for a held and for a failed job (P2, P25, R-A1).

**`review_action`.** Phase 2 implements one review action, `approve` with no overrides, which accepts unchanged the reasons the job was held with (P25); `lazymkvctl approve JOB` sends it. **`rip_again`.** Names a job past its disc phase and delivers RipAgain; the next insertion of that disc starts a new job.

**`shutdown`** follows part 3.8, and the engine sends its `ok` before it exits. **`command`** follows part 9.5.

### 9.5 Plan, decide and retune (P23)

`lazymkvctl` decides where these commands run by what is named.

- **A path or slug: `lazymkvctl` runs the command itself.** Given a recording folder on a reachable path, or a slug under the evidence clone, it runs `plan`, `decide` or `retune` through the `dev` crate's library, handing its words to the dispatcher `lazymkv-dev` uses, so the documents and exit codes are identical. This needs no engine and no elevation and works on any OS. `lazymkv-dev` keeps these commands, and their goldens stay unchanged.
- **A job: the engine runs it.** Given `--job ID` (for `plan`, and for `decide` with `--title KEY`) or `--jobs` (for `retune`), `lazymkvctl` sends `command`. The engine starts its sibling `lazymkvctl.exe` from its run folder as a child process, under ProcessGuard at below-normal priority, in the desktop session where the share and the tuning evidence are reachable. It streams the child's lines back as `command_output` and replies `ok` with the child's exit code; `lazymkvctl` prints the lines and exits with that code. One command runs at a time. The engine refuses a second command, or one naming an unknown job, with an error reply, and `lazymkvctl` then exits 2, never 1, since exit 1 is a reconcile no for `decide` and a FAIL for `retune`, and `plan` never uses it (RP24).

Before it builds the child's words, the engine fetches again any TMDb copy the child will read that is more than six months old or was deleted by the cleanup (part 8.4). When it cannot, the command exits 3 with the reason and leaves the job's state alone (K15). The engine then builds the words from the job folder `<jobs>\<ID>` under `%ProgramData%\lazymkv\jobs`, adding each optional flag, `--tmdb-record` among them, only when its file exists after that refetch, and passes every other word unchanged (designer's call; no builder call):

## 10. Subtitle bitmaps and OCR

### 10.1 What this part settles

Spec 6.4 step 10 leaves to phase 2 how the app decodes the bitmap subtitles and finds the text lines, and spec 7 leaves the form of the OCR evidence. This part settles both, with P3, P17 to P20 and P26 and the builder calls OCR-2, OCR-3, OCR-6, OCR-9, OCR-13 and EVID-5; K2's worker process supersedes OCR-12's reading pool. Where a builder recommendation met an answer, the answer won: OCR-1 asked whether the engine runs OCR at all, and P3 says it does; P18 adopts OCR-4's detector for irregular events and settles OCR-7 by keeping both models; P17 and P26 set OCR-5's speed bar; P19 answers OCR-10's failed read; and P20 replaces the model crates of OCR-8 and ARCH-8 with a download at build.

Nothing here changes `core`'s text rule, which stays EXP-14's, ported as `core::subtitle::text`. Until experiment 14 answers yes, the text only adds `subtitle_ambiguous` reviews with the note `subtitle_text_disagrees` (D20, spec 8.7 "Text"). The work splits across two crates (R-A5): the Matroska reader, the decoders, the bitmap preparation and the row line finder are pure functions of bytes in `core::subtitle::bitmap`, new in phase 2, and ocrs, its embedded models and the OCR worker `lazymkv-ocr.exe` live in the `ocr` crate (parts 4.1 and 10.7, K2).

### 10.2 Where OCR runs

OCR runs in every job at spec 6.4 step 10, wherever that step runs (P3). `core::tracks::subtitle::step10_runs` decides: the title must have two or more English or no-language subtitle streams in the file, counting the derived forced-only streams and the closed captions, which is the wording of the phase 1 notes and the code; part 13 aligns the spec's "two or more English subtitle streams" to it. The reading does not depend on `keep_tuning_evidence`, which decides only whether the text and the bitmaps are also kept under `tuning_root` (part 10.12). Step 10 runs in this order:

1. mkvmerge writes the subtitle-only evidence file to `<staging>\<job id>\evidence\<title key>.mks` (spec 5, unchanged).
2. ffprobe lists each stream's packets into the job's recording as `subs\sub-<n>.packets.json`, as in phase 0 (P21); spec 8.7's counts and start times still come from this listing.
3. The engine starts the OCR worker once for each English or no-language PGS or VobSub stream, one stream at a time (part 10.7, K2). It gives each run the staged `evidence.mks` path, the stream id, `sub_clear_max_bytes` from the job's settings and the output folder `<staging>\<job id>\tuning\<title key>\subs-ocr\` (R-A6). A text stream is set aside unread, as spec 8.7 says, which covers the DVD closed captions MakeMKV converts to SubRip; streams in other languages are not read.
4. Each run reads the evidence file into memory, hands the bytes to the Matroska reader (part 10.3) and reads its one stream (parts 10.4 to 10.7). It writes the stream's `sub-<n>.ocr.jsonl`, one record per event with its `pts`, decoded size, method, trigger and stage times; the stream's totals in `sub-<n>.run.json`; and the stream's crops, then exits (part 10.12, L6).
5. After each run the engine checks the recorded events against ffprobe's `subs\sub-<n>.packets.json` (part 10.3). Once every stream's run has ended, it runs `core::subtitle::text::subs_run` over the text with the recording's packets, writes `subs-ocr\sub-<n>.frames.json` for each read stream from the worker's records and the text rule's markers, writes `subs-ocr\summary.json` from the workers' `sub-<n>.run.json` files and the text rule's markers (part 10.12), and passes the decisions to `decide_subtitles`.

Step 10 finishes reading before step 10a's audio decode starts, in the spec's order.

### 10.3 Reading evidence.mks

The OCR worker reads each event's payload and the VobSub palette from the evidence file itself, with no further tool in the bundle [OCR-2]. The reader is a small EBML reader of our own rather than a crate, because the file has one narrow, known shape and no Matroska crate was verified in phase 0. It takes the segment's timestamp scale; for each track its number, `CodecID` (`S_HDMV/PGS` or `S_VOBSUB`), `CodecPrivate` and content encodings; and each cluster's timestamp with its simple blocks or block groups. For VobSub, `CodecPrivate` holds the idx header, which gives the frame size and the 16-colour palette. mkvmerge may store a track with zlib compression or header stripping, and it zlib-compresses PGS and VobSub tracks by default. Step 10's extraction keeps that default, so phase 0's evidence files and the engine's read alike (K1). The reader undoes both encodings with `miniz_oxide` (part 4.7), refuses any other content encoding, and refuses laced blocks.

The worker records each event's `pts` and its size, once the content encoding is undone, in `sub-<n>.ocr.jsonl` (part 10.12). After the worker's run, the engine checks those events against ffprobe's listing of the same stream in the recording's `subs\sub-<n>.packets.json`, whose events are the packets larger than `sub_clear_max_bytes`: they must agree in number, order and size. The plan's first golden check confirms that the sizes compare once the content encoding is undone. ffprobe lists the sizes after undoing the encoding only when FFmpeg is built with zlib, which is why the minimal build enables it (part 11.2, K1). Any disagreement leaves the stream unread (part 10.8). The cross-check keeps spec 8.7's event counts and the events the OCR reads in step.

### 10.4 Decoding PGS and VobSub

The decoders are our own code, pure functions over the reader's packets, with no dependency beyond the zlib inflater [OCR-3]. A packet larger than `sub_clear_max_bytes`, which the engine gives the worker from the job's settings (part 10.2), is an event, as in spec 8.7; smaller packets clear the screen, are not decoded and produce no record.

- **PGS.** A block is one display set: composition, window, palette, object and end segments. The decoder keeps the epoch's palettes and objects across blocks, because a later display set may reuse an object defined earlier; it joins an object that spans several object segments, decodes the run-length data and applies the composition's cropping. Palette entries are Y, Cr, Cb and alpha.
- **VobSub.** A block is one subpicture unit. The decoder reads its control sequence for the four colour indices, the four alpha values, the display area and the two interlaced field offsets, decodes the run-length data of both fields, and looks the colours up in the idx palette, taking Y from the RGB values by BT.601.

An event decodes to a list of positioned objects, each with indexed pixels, the palette and alpha. A decoding error leaves the stream unread (part 10.8).

The tests run in two layers. On CI's three systems, hand-built PGS display sets and VobSub units in the test code exercise every branch: run-length codes, epoch reuse, multi-segment objects, cropping, interlaced fields, zlib content and the refusals; their shapes are made up, so no glyph from any disc enters the repository. The golden tests compare the decoder with ffmpeg's phase 0 output: for each of the six phase 0 tracks of part 10.9, every event of the track's evidence file goes through part 10.5's preparation, is placed on the canvas size phase 0 used, and is compared with ffmpeg's render of the same event, the negated grey frame that `ocr-subs.ps1` kept in the tuning dataset. Events and frames are paired as `core::subtitle::text` pairs phase 0's records. An event passes when its dark-pixel box equals the frame's exactly and no pixel differs by more than 2 grey levels, which allows for ffmpeg's rounding through RGB; each run reports the largest difference found. These tests need the tuning dataset (`LAZYMKV_OCR_TEXT`) and the recordings (`LAZYMKV_EVIDENCE`), skip without them as DEV-4's private-corpus tests do, run on the mini PC in the owner's desktop session, the only session that reaches the share, and print numbers only.

### 10.5 Preparing the bitmap and reading each distinct bitmap once

The preparation is the same operation as phase 0's ffmpeg chain, overlay on black, negate, grey [OCR-13]: each object pixel becomes `255 - round(Y * alpha / 255)`, composited onto white, so the text comes out dark and black outlines vanish into the white, as in phase 0. The prepared event is cropped to the box of its dark pixels (grey 127 or lower), grown by 16 pixels on each side as in phase 0's crop trial; the crop is the image the line finder and ocrs see. Grey loses colour, but the kept evidence file keeps it for a later colour measure, such as one for UK-style SDH (EXP-14 section 8, question 5).

Every event is decoded, and each distinct crop is read once [OCR-9]. The key is a SHA-1 of the crop's size and pixels; SHA-1 is already a dependency of `core` and is used here only to find identical bitmaps. A repeat takes the text of its first occurrence. This keeps EXP-14's rule of whole tracks with no sampling, while skipping work that cannot change a measure. The crops of one stream are held in memory while it is read; at phase 0's mean box of 611 x 143 pixels, a dense film track of 1,700 events needs about 150 MB.

### 10.6 Finding the lines (P18)

ocrs's recognition runs on line boxes found from the crop's rows, and ocrs's detector runs only on irregular events.

- **Rows.** A row is inked when it holds a pixel of grey 127 or lower, and a line is a maximal run of inked rows; a gap of 2 or more empty rows separates two lines. Each line box spans its run's inked columns, padded by 16 pixels within the crop.
- **Irregular events.** An event goes to ocrs's detector, through `detect_words`, `find_text_lines` and `recognize_text` on the crop, when any of these holds: (1) no clear gap: a run is taller than 1.6 times the stream's median line height, taken over its one- and two-line events; (2) a tall line: any single line exceeds that limit; (3) many lines: the rows give more than 3 lines; (4) top and bottom text: the event has ink both above and below the middle row of the video frame.
- **Constants.** The ink level 127, the gap of 2 rows, the 16-pixel padding, the factor 1.6, the 3 lines and the middle row are fixed in the code, not settings. Every record names the line finder's version (part 10.12), and a change to any constant after the trial of part 10.9 needs a new trial.

Both models stay embedded, about 12.2 MB in all, because the detector serves the irregular events (P18; this settles OCR-7 as D22 has it). The trial counts how many events each of the four triggers sends to the detector, from each record's `trigger` (part 10.12, L6).

### 10.7 Recognition and the OCR worker

Each regular event is read with ocrs's `recognize_text`, each line given as one rectangle; a record's lines are ocrs's text lines in top-to-bottom order. ocrs is pinned at `=0.13.1` and rten at `=0.26.0` [ARCH-13]. The models are loaded once per process from the embedded bytes with `rten::Model::load_static_slice`.

ocrs and rten accept no thread pool: both models run on rten's own global rayon pool, sized by `RTEN_NUM_THREADS` or else the physical core count, with no hook to set its threads' priority. The reading therefore runs in a worker process, `lazymkv-ocr.exe`, the `ocr` crate's binary (K2). The engine starts its sibling `lazymkv-ocr.exe` from its own folder through `ProcessGuard`, at below-normal priority, the priority spec 6 gives the file phase's tools. It sets `RTEN_NUM_THREADS=3` in the worker's environment, the J4125's four cores less one. It runs the worker once for each stream it reads, giving it the staged `evidence.mks` path, the stream id, `sub_clear_max_bytes` and the output folder (part 10.2). Each run reads one English or no-language PGS or VobSub stream, writes its `sub-<n>.ocr.jsonl`, its `sub-<n>.run.json` and EVID-5's crops (part 10.12, L6), and exits. Only one stream is read at a time: the engine starts the next run once the last has ended, and `file_phase_concurrency` is 1. Cancelling the job kills the worker and deletes its partial files [ENG-13]. `lazymkv-dev ocr-trial` runs the same worker, so the trial measures what ships (part 10.9). K2 supersedes OCR-12's reading pool inside the engine.

### 10.8 The time cap and the note `subtitle_text_unread` (P19)

Each stream has a time cap of three times its expected time. The expected time is P26's bar, 600 s for Pan's Labyrinth's English track of 887 events, about 676 ms per event, so the cap is 2.03 s per event, about 2 s, and a floor of 60 s per stream keeps a short forced track from being cut off by start-up costs (P26). The clock is the wall time of the stream's worker run, from its start to its exit, the model load included; the 60 s floor covers that start-up. The engine enforces the cap: once a stream's run passes it, the engine kills that worker, discards its partial output and goes on to the next stream (K2). The cap rests on the bar, not on a measured rate, so it does not move between builds; it is a fixed value, not a setting, and joins spec 14's list of fixed values.

A stream is unread when its reading passes the cap; when the reader refuses its encoding or lacing, or its packets disagree with ffprobe's (part 10.3); when its codec is a bitmap codec other than PGS or VobSub; when the decoder fails; or when ocrs returns an error. An unread stream's partial text and crops are deleted (P19). The job gets the note `subtitle_text_unread`, new in phase 2 and defined here once. Its detail names the stream and the reason as a fact, with no subtitle text: `stream {n} was not read: {reason}`, where the reason is `time cap of {s} s reached after {k} of {m} events` or the error. The note never holds the job, and no review reason is raised.

Because the text rule judges a title's tracks together, `subs_run` refuses a title in which an English or no-language bitmap stream has no text, so that title's text adds no review and spec 8.7 decides on counts and timing alone; the note makes this visible. The other streams' complete text is still kept as evidence. The note reaches the decision through `core`: `core::tracks::subtitle::SubtitleFacts` gains the unread streams with their reasons, `decide` turns them into `Note::SubtitleTextUnread` with the detail, and the dev reader fills them from `subs-ocr\summary.json`, so the engine and `lazymkvctl decide` agree (designer's call; no builder call). The closed set of notes grows from thirteen to fourteen: spec 8.7 gains the note, and `core::review::Note` gains `SubtitleTextUnread`.

Missing either brings in Tesseract (part 10.10). The plan makes the trial the first task of the OCR pull request (part 12.6), after the decoder's golden tests pass and before the engine calls the reader. It uses the six distinct tracks phase 0 read with OCR, with each track's events counted, as spec 8.7 counts them, from its recording's packet list:

The protocol:

1. **Inventory.** In the desktop session, check that each track has its frames and Tesseract reading in the tuning dataset; its evidence file, from the dataset copy or else the phase 0 staging path the recording's `summary.json` names under `evidence`; and its recording's `subs\sub-<n>.packets.json`. 2. **Run F** on the five tracks that need it, one at a time, with `ocrs-trial.ps1` started through phase 0's `job.sh`.
3. **Run R and R'** with `lazymkv-dev ocr-trial`, new in phase 2, started through `job.sh` in the desktop session; for R it runs the OCR worker as the engine does (K2). `job.sh` refuses to start while another phase 0 job runs, and the builder also checks that no `makemkvcon` or cargo process runs before the timed run. This reads P17's "measures match" as equal counts and same-side shares, a reading the owner accepted (P34; designer's call; no builder call).

**Speed passes** when Pan's Labyrinth's English track, the phase 0 two-hour film track of 887 events, reads through R in 600 s or less, about 676 ms per event (P26). The mean wall time per event over all six tracks is reported as information, not as a second condition.

**One trial.** The builder runs the trial once, on the build the plan names, and a fail is final unless the owner allows a second. The method's constants are fixed before the run (part 10.6), so the result cannot be tuned to the six tracks it is judged on.

### 10.10 If the trial fails: Tesseract

If either half fails, the plan turns to D22's fallback [OCR-6]: a statically built `tesseract.exe` with the `eng` model from tessdata_fast, both Apache-2.0, shipped in `resources\` beside ffmpeg and mkvmerge; one subprocess per stream, given a list file of the same prepared crops of part 10.5 and called with psm 6 as in phase 0; and the same time cap, note and evidence form. The fallback is not built before a fail. On a fail the OCR pull request stops, and the builder brings the static build route, its licence audit and the bundle's contents to the owner before anything is built for shipping or installed. Tesseract 5.4.0 on the mini PC stays phase 0's measuring tool. If Tesseract replaces ocrs, the models of part 10.11 leave the build and the release.

### 10.11 The model files (P20)

The build fetches the two models, and nothing fetches them at run time (D17).

- **Build.** The `ocr` crate's `build.rs` downloads `text-detection.onnx` (2,499,479 bytes) and `text-recognition.onnx` (9,713,177 bytes) from ocrs's bucket at the pinned URLs `https://ocrs-models.s3-accelerate.amazonaws.com/text-detection.onnx` and `.../text-recognition.onnx`, writes them to `OUT_DIR` and embeds them with `include_bytes!`. Each file must match its pinned SHA-256, and a mismatch fails the build. The plan computes the two SHA-256 values from the files whose SHA-1 matches phase 0's and writes them into the crate.
- **Local copy.** The environment variable `LAZYMKV_OCRS_MODELS`, new in phase 2, names a folder holding both files; when it is set, the build takes them from there and checks the same SHA-256 values. This covers offline builds and the mini PC, which already holds the files in `C:\lazymkv\phase0\models`, through `box.sh`'s environment. CI keeps the downloaded files in its cache under a key made of the two hashes and passes that folder as the local copy.
- **Release.** The release job uploads the exact embedded bytes, checked against the pins, to every lazymkv release, with the CC-BY-SA-4.0 licence text and the attribution (part 11.4); in phase 2's dry run they stay CI artifacts (P28). If the bucket moves, the URL is repointed at any release's copy.
- **Commands.** `lazymkvctl licenses` prints the attribution and licence, and `lazymkvctl export-models <dir>` writes the embedded files out unmodified, standing in for D22's `lazymkv` commands until phase 5 [ARCH-17].

### 10.12 OCR evidence and where text lives

The OCR evidence keeps phase 0's file shape [EVID-5/OCR-11] in the two trees of EVID-2, and the files are defined once here. For each read stream `<n>`, the text sits in the dataset tree only:

- `sub-<n>.ocr.jsonl`: one record per event, in `pts` order, with phase 0's keys `frame` (the event's index among the stream's events), `file` (the crop's PNG, shared by repeats), `t` and `pts` (both the event's start in seconds, from its block's timestamp, which equals the packet's `pts_time` in `sub-<n>.packets.json`; phase 0's `t` was its rendered frame's time, and the text rule reads `pts` first), `lines` (an array, never joined) and `text` (the lines joined with ` | `, for older readers). The extra keys are `size` (the event's packet size once the content encoding is undone, which the engine's cross-check of part 10.3 compares), `boxes` (the line boxes within the crop), `method` (`rows` or `detector`), `trigger` (null for `rows`; for `detector`, 1 to 4, the number of the first of part 10.6's four triggers that holds), `engine` (the ocrs version and both models' SHA-256), `decoder` (the decoder's and line finder's versions) and `ms`. `ms` is an object of the event's milliseconds per stage: `decode` (reading and decoding the event), `prepare` (part 10.5's preparation), `lines` (the row line finder), `detect` (the detector, null when it did not run) and `recognize` (L6). The core reader keeps extra keys in `OcrLine`'s `rest`.
- `sub-<n>.run.json`: the stream's totals, which the worker writes when it has read the stream: the events, the distinct crops, the events by method and, for the detector, by trigger, the total of each of the five `ms` stages, the model versions as `engine` gives them, and the run's wall time (L6). It holds no text.
- `s<n>\f-<pts>.png`: one 8-bit grey PNG per distinct crop, exactly as ocrs read it, named by its first event's pts.
- `evidence.mks`: a copy of the title's subtitle evidence file, with the bitmaps and their palettes.

The recording gets counts only, never text (spec 7, 15). `subs-ocr\sub-<n>.frames.json`, which the engine writes for each read stream from the worker's `sub-<n>.ocr.jsonl` and the text rule's markers, has one entry per event with phase 0's seven keys, `frame`, `file`, `t`, `pts`, `chars`, `lines` (a count) and `markers`. `subs-ocr\summary.json`, which the engine writes once every stream's run has ended, from each read stream's `sub-<n>.run.json` and the text rule's markers, gives, per stream: the events and distinct crops; the events read by rows and by the detector, per trigger; the events with text, the lines and the markers; the stage totals and the wall time; the versions; and whether the stream was read or why not. When a stream is unread, no `.ocr.jsonl` is kept for it, and `summary.json` says why (P19).

Where the text lives:

1. **Staging.** During the job, the text and crops are in `<staging>\<job id>\tuning\<title key>\subs-ocr\` (R-A6), and they are deleted with the job's raw data after `retention_days` (P4).
2. **Tuning.** While `keep_tuning_evidence` is on, the folder is copied to `<tuning_root>\dataset\<job id>\<title key>\subs-ocr\` and the counts to `<tuning_root>\recordings\<job id>\<title key>\subs-ocr\`, with EVID-4's discipline of `.partial`, check, rename. Only the owner deletes them.
3. **Never elsewhere.** No text is written to `%ProgramData%\lazymkv\jobs\`, a `decide.json`, a notification or the IPC snapshot; the text rule's decisions and notes carry roles, classes and counts only.
4. **Never in a repository.** No engine job recording or OCR text is committed to `lazymkv-evidence` (P22); only the dated live lookup records, by the owner's hand (part 8.5, R-B3). A later pull script would copy recordings, never the dataset tree. `.gitignore` already keeps `*.ocr.jsonl` and the whole `corpus/owner/` clone out of this repository.

### 11.2 The minimal FFmpeg build (P16)

P16 fixes the components: the matroska demuxer; decoders and parsers for TrueHD and MLP, DTS (`dca`), AC-3, E-AC-3, AAC, MP2, `pcm_bluray`, `pcm_dvd`, `pcm_s16le`, `pcm_s24le`, `pcm_s32le` and FLAC; the FLAC encoder; the matroska, null and streamhash muxers; the filters `pan`, `aresample`, `highpass`, `lowpass`, `asetnsamples`, `astats` and `ametadata`, with `aformat` and `anull`, which the ffmpeg program pulls in itself; and the file protocol. The build leaves out every video decoder, networking, libavdevice, libswscale and libpostproc, and links one library from outside FFmpeg, zlib, which K1 adds below.

Phase 0's command lines need three things P16's list does not name. The level and proxy graph splits each decoded stream with `asplit`. The proxy chain, and `levels-from-proxy`'s undoing of the stored gain, use `volume`. The null muxer's default audio encoder is `pcm_s16le`, so without that encoder `-f null` fails, which the first build confirms. This design adds `asplit`, `volume` and the `pcm_s16le` encoder, all LGPL. FFmpeg registers the fixed-point `mp2` decoder before `mp2float`, so the reference build already decoded MP2 with `mp2`, and P16's `mp2` suffices. The ffmpeg program needs a thread library, and `--disable-autodetect` turns off auto-detection, so the build enables native Windows threads explicitly and links no pthreads library. The same switch leaves out zlib, which the Matroska demuxer needs to undo the zlib compression mkvmerge applies by default to PGS and VobSub tracks; without it `ffprobe` lists the compressed packet sizes of step 10's evidence file. The build therefore adds `--enable-zlib`, linking zlib built from its pinned source archive (part 11.3), under zlib's own licence (K1). The owner confirmed these four additions to P16's list: `asplit`, `volume`, the `pcm_s16le` encoder and `--enable-zlib` (P30).

The configure line in the build script has this form; the script in the repository is the authority:

```
./configure --enable-cross-compile --target-os=mingw32 --arch=x86_64 \
  --cross-prefix=x86_64-w64-mingw32- \
  --extra-ldflags="-static -Wl,--no-insert-timestamp" \
  --disable-autodetect --enable-w32threads --enable-zlib \
  --disable-everything --disable-doc --disable-network \
  --disable-avdevice --disable-swscale \
  --disable-programs --enable-ffmpeg --enable-ffprobe \
  --enable-demuxer=matroska \
  --enable-decoder=truehd,mlp,dca,ac3,eac3,aac,mp2,pcm_bluray,pcm_dvd,pcm_s16le,pcm_s24le,pcm_s32le,flac \
  --enable-parser=mlp,dca,ac3,aac,mpegaudio,flac \
  --enable-encoder=flac,pcm_s16le \
  --enable-muxer=matroska,null,streamhash \
  --enable-filter=pan,aresample,highpass,lowpass,asetnsamples,astats,ametadata,aformat,anull,asplit,volume \
  --enable-protocol=file
```

The build never passes `--enable-gpl`, `--enable-version3` or `--enable-nonfree`, and `--disable-autodetect` keeps out every other library the runner happens to have, so the two executables, built from FFmpeg's own source and zlib, are LGPL-2.1-or-later, with zlib under its own permissive licence (K1). `-Wl,--no-insert-timestamp` stops GNU ld from stamping the link time into the PE images, so that a rebuild reproduces the same bytes (part 11.3, K4). They keep x86 assembly, as the reference build had it; FFmpeg picks SSE paths at run time on the J4125, which has no AVX2. Three consequences follow: `ffprobe` still lists video and subtitle streams from the container without decoding them, which the reference run checks; the bitmap goldens of OCR-3 come from the full FFmpeg on the mini PC, since this build has no subtitle decoders [OCR-3]; and CI's test that builds a proxy through `lavfi` keeps using the runner's own FFmpeg.

### 11.3 The FFmpeg CI build job

The build has its own workflow, separate from the test workflow; everything in this subsection is the designer's call with no builder call, except the manifest [ARCH-13].

- **Inputs.** A `packaging/` folder, new in phase 2, holds the tools manifest [ARCH-13] and the FFmpeg build script. The manifest gives the FFmpeg version, the tarball's URL on ffmpeg.org and its SHA-256; the URL and SHA-256 of the source archive of the zlib the build links (K1); beside these tarball pins, the version of each apt package the runner installs; the MKVToolNix portable archive's URL and SHA-256; the model URLs and SHA-256s (P20); and the SHA-256s of the verified `ffmpeg.exe` and `ffprobe.exe`.
- **Runner.** A pinned Ubuntu runner image installs the apt packages of the mingw-w64 cross-compiler, binutils and nasm, each at the version the build script pins as `package=version` and the manifest records, on the runner only, as the owner approved (P33, part 4.7); nothing is installed on the owner's machines. zlib comes from no apt package: the build compiles it from its pinned source archive (K1).
- **Steps.** Download the FFmpeg tarball and the zlib source archive and check their SHA-256s before unpacking, failing on a mismatch. Set `SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH` to the pinned FFmpeg tarball's date, build zlib from its archive with the cross-compiler, then configure and build FFmpeg with the script, whose `-Wl,--no-insert-timestamp` keeps the link time out of the images, so two builds of the same inputs are byte-identical (K4). Write the build settings file: the script, the configure line as run, the versions of GCC, binutils, nasm and zlib, and FFmpeg's configure summary.
- **Smoke test.** A Windows job runs `-version` for each executable, `ffmpeg -hide_banner -layouts`, and the shapes of phase 0's level, proxy and streamhash command lines over a small Matroska file the runner's full FFmpeg makes, so a missing component fails here, not on a disc.
- **When it runs.** Only on a push that changes the FFmpeg pin or the build script, or when started by hand. The workflow is also callable from another workflow (`workflow_call`).
- **Where the binaries live.** The first build is kept as a workflow artifact, and the one-off check on the mini PC runs on that artifact; the manifest then records its two SHA-256s (K4). A release, and the dry run of P28, rebuild: the release workflow's Linux job calls the build workflow, which builds again from the same inputs, and passes the binaries as an artifact to the Windows job. Release step 3 fails unless both SHA-256s equal the manifest's, so a release ships the very bytes the check verified (part 11.5).
- **The one-off check on the mini PC (P16, R-A2).** The two executables of the build workflow's artifact are copied into a run folder on the mini PC, as DEV-16 does for lazymkv's own binaries, with the owner's yes. The check runs in the desktop session with that folder first on `PATH`, so both `lazymkv-dev` and phase 0's `audio-levels.ps1`, whose `env.ps1` takes the first `ffmpeg.exe` on `PATH`, use them. It runs `lazymkv-dev golden check`; step 10a's measures over the kept Pan's Labyrinth rip [DEV-3], made with `audio-levels.ps1` and compared with phase 0's level files, stream hashes and `ffprobe` listings, the profile and channel-layout spellings included; and `retune` over the phase 0 corpus with the new level files. It also runs a step 10 listing: the minimal `ffprobe`'s packet list of a phase 0 `evidence.mks` must equal the full build's, which shows that zlib-compressed tracks are listed at their decoded sizes (K1). Identical numbers pass the build, and the manifest then records the artifact's two SHA-256s (K4). Any difference stops the check, the differing values go to the owner, and no build ships until the owner decides.
- **Later upgrades.** A later change of the FFmpeg pin repeats the whole check; P16's "once" applies to each pin. A change of the toolchain or of any pinned package needs a new one-off check on the mini PC too, before the manifest's SHA-256s change.

### 11.4 Licence duties (P13)

lazymkv stays MIT. It drives `mkvmerge`, `ffmpeg` and `ffprobe` as separate programs through command lines (spec 4.2 `tools`) and links no code of theirs. Every GitHub release carries these assets: the MKVToolNix 100.0.0 source archive; the FFmpeg 9.0.2 source tarball; the source archive of the zlib that `ffmpeg.exe` and `ffprobe.exe` link (K1); the FFmpeg build settings file; the two model files, with exactly the bytes embedded in that release's executables, and the CC-BY-SA attribution (P20); and a `SHA256SUMS` file covering every asset. The release job downloads each source from its pinned URL and checks its SHA-256, so every release attaches the same bytes until a pin changes. The source sits in the same place as the binaries, which meets GPL-2's "equivalent access" without a written offer, so the owner has no requests to answer in later years.

The release's `licenses\` folder holds lazymkv's MIT licence; GPL-2.0 for mkvmerge; LGPL-2.1 and the build settings for FFmpeg, and the zlib licence for the zlib linked into it (K1); the CC-BY-SA-4.0 text and the attribution for the models; the notices of the Rust crates compiled into the executables, ocrs and rten among them; the notices of the mingw-w64 runtime parts statically linked into `ffmpeg.exe` and `ffprobe.exe`; and a list of the source assets by name and SHA-256. The release job generates the crate notices from `Cargo.lock` with `cargo-about`, a tool the owner approved with the crates (P33, part 4.7) [ENG-7/DEV-11]. The model attribution names the ocrs project's text-detection and text-recognition models and their CC-BY-SA-4.0 licence, gives the Hugging Face card as the source, says that they were trained on HierText (CC BY-SA 4.0) and on synthetic data, and says that lazymkv ships them unmodified.

A `cargo install` has no `licenses\` folder, so `lazymkvctl` carries the texts itself [ARCH-17]: `lazymkvctl licenses` prints them (part 9.6), and `lazymkvctl export-models <dir>` writes the two unmodified model files, so no technological measure restricts them (D22). The phase 5 UI program adds the same two commands.

One point is contested. The portable `mkvmerge.exe` also contains libraries that MKVToolNix's Windows build links statically, and P13 names only the MKVToolNix source archive. Whether GPL-2's "complete corresponding source" also needs those libraries' sources is not settled here and goes to the owner before the first public release (P28, part 15).

### 11.5 The release layout (P24)

Phase 2 fixes the layout and builds it as a zip, which stays a CI artifact until the first release (P28). Phase 5 chooses the installer tool and adds the logon task (spec 4.1), and the installer installs the same tree.

```
lazymkv-<version>-windows-x64.zip
  lazymkv-<version>\
    bin\
      lazymkv-engine.exe
      lazymkv-ocr.exe        (models embedded; the OCR worker the engine starts, part 10.7)

      mkvmerge.exe
```

The engine finds `resources\` beside its own `bin\` folder, so the tree works wherever it is extracted. The zip is meant for a folder that only administrators can write, such as one under Program Files; in a folder standard users can write, the engine skips `resources\` (part 3.6), and a missing tool shows in the self-test. `%ProgramData%\lazymkv`, with the settings, jobs and remembered decisions, is created by the engine and is never part of the zip.

**Users who build with cargo** build `lazymkv-engine`, `lazymkv-ocr` and `lazymkvctl` with `cargo install --git`, since the workspace does not publish to crates.io. Their binaries embed the models, which `build.rs` fetches at build time or takes from a local copy (P20). They get no `resources\`: they install MKVToolNix, FFmpeg and MakeMKV themselves and name the tools through the tool-path settings or a `PATH` folder that only administrators can write (D22, MISS-ARCH-18). The self-test reports a missing tool, or one below the minimum versions of part 11.1. The tuned thresholds were measured with the pinned builds, so another FFmpeg may move a measure; each job records the version of every tool it ran [EVID-10].

**The release job** is built in phase 2 and runs only as a dry run on a branch (P28). Its zip, the source archives and the model files stay CI artifacts, and no tag is pushed and nothing is public until the owner says so. From the first release on, it runs when a tag `v<version>` is pushed, and the owner pushes the tag or approves each push. It runs on a Windows runner fed by the Linux job of part 11.3, and it is the only job with `contents: write`; the test workflow stays read-only. Its steps:

1. Build `lazymkv-engine.exe`, `lazymkv-ocr.exe` and `lazymkvctl.exe` with `--locked --release`, with `build.rs` checking the model SHA-256s.
2. Download the MKVToolNix portable archive, check its SHA-256, extract only `mkvmerge.exe`, and run `mkvmerge --version` from `resources\` outside any installation [ARCH-19].
3. Take the `ffmpeg.exe` and `ffprobe.exe` the build workflow rebuilt, and fail unless their SHA-256s equal the manifest's (part 11.3, K4).
4. Assemble the tree and the licences.
5. Run the signing step, empty for now (part 11.6).
6. Zip the tree, write `SHA256SUMS`, and create the release with the zip and the assets of part 11.4; the dry run uploads the same files as CI artifacts instead (P28).

**Development on the mini PC.** The phase 2 engine there uses the tools already installed (P24): MakeMKV 2.0.0, mkvmerge v100.0 (MKVToolNix 100.0.0, the pinned version [ARCH-19]) and FFmpeg 9.0.2, the full gyan.dev build. Part 3.6 gives the settings: `ffprobe` and `ffmpeg` by path, `makemkvcon` and `mkvmerge` from their default install folders. Only the check of part 11.3 runs the CI-built FFmpeg there (R-A2).

### 11.6 Signing (P14, P15)

**Phase 2 is unsigned (P15).** Nothing is bought or applied for, and the release notes say the release is unsigned. On a machine with Smart App Control on, the engine, `lazymkvctl` and the self-built FFmpeg may be blocked, depending on Microsoft's cloud verdict for each file; in phase 0 it blocked `cargo` but let an unsigned `lazymkv-capture.exe` run. Windows SmartScreen also warns on downloads. The mini PC has had Smart App Control off since 2026-10-06, so development and acceptance are not affected.

**The empty step.** The release job's signing step sits after the tree is assembled and before it is zipped, and for now only logs that the release is unsigned under P15. Once a certificate exists, it signs every `.exe` in `bin\` and `resources\` with an RFC 3161 timestamp, the bundled tools included, which P14 says are re-signed. The manifest pins the unsigned bytes; signing changes the files, so `SHA256SUMS` lists the bytes that actually ship. When a bundled file already carries an upstream signature, the step adds lazymkv's signature beside it rather than replacing it (designer's call; no builder call). Re-signing is possible with Artifact Signing or the owner's own certificate; SignPath's terms do not allow it.

**The Microsoft Store is rejected (P15).** Microsoft's packaging guidance says that apps "that require elevation for any part of their functionality won't be accepted", and the engine must run elevated because MakeMKV needs administrator rights to reach the drives. The two technical routes around this are closed in practice: `allowElevation` is a restricted capability for partners and enterprises under strict criteria, and packaged services are not approved in most cases. The Store has further problems for lazymkv: MSIX may virtualise HKCU writes, which would put `makemkvcon`'s registry writes in a private copy, and Microsoft's pages contradict each other on this; starting a packaged program elevated from a scheduled task is not documented; every update goes through certification, up to about three business days; and there is no plain direct download. The Store's MSI/EXE route avoids MSIX but needs the developer's own signature from a Trusted Root Program CA, so it saves nothing.

Smart App Control lets a file run on a valid signature from a Trusted Root Program CA when the cloud has no verdict on it, with no reputation needed. ## 12. Testing, development process and the pull requests

Phase 2 is integration work. Its tests must show that the engine drives real tools, real Windows facilities and live services correctly, while staying fast and repeatable on CI machines with no drive, no share and no token. The design uses two test layers, a small set of hardware tests on the mini PC, and pull requests that each end with a running job.

### 12.1 Two test layers

The first layer is whole jobs on stand-ins, and it runs everywhere [DEV-3]. Each job runs from a media-arrival event to a filed file using stand-ins for `makemkvcon`, `mkvmerge`, `ffprobe` and `ffmpeg`; in-memory fakes for `DriveWatcher`, `Ejector`, `ShareConnector`, `SecretStore`, `KeepAwake` and `IpcTransport` and for the helpers of part 4.2 [ARCH-1]; the recorded-answer transport for the clients (part 8.1); and a fixture `appdata.tar` (part 5.4). The disc is a temporary folder of small files laid out like the scenario's file listing, so spec 6.1 step 2's fingerprint reads real bytes; the output files are placeholders. These tests run on ubuntu, macos and windows through the existing `cargo test --workspace --locked` and cover every path the reducer can take in phase 2.

The second layer runs real tools on small files and skips where they are absent [DEV-3]. Real `mkvmerge`, `ffprobe` and `ffmpeg` run on short Matroska files that `ffmpeg`'s lavfi sources generate during the test, which catches wrong command lines, wrong parsing of real output and verification mistakes that replay cannot. The tests find each tool through `LAZYMKV_TEST_MKVMERGE`, `LAZYMKV_TEST_FFPROBE` and `LAZYMKV_TEST_FFMPEG`, test-only variables new in phase 2, else plain `PATH`, and a test whose tool is missing prints `skipped: <reason>` and passes, as the clone tests do (designer's call; no builder call). CI already installs `ffmpeg` on all three runners, and installs MKVToolNix through the same package managers, a step the owner approved with the direct crates (P33, part 4.7). The tests skip where the installed FFmpeg is below 7.0, part 11.1's minimum, as on CI's ubuntu runner, which ships 6.1; they run on the Windows and macOS runners' copies or the mini PC's (K11). On the mini PC the cargo command line sets the three variables, as it sets `LAZYMKV_EVIDENCE`, to the installed mkvmerge v100.0, ffprobe 9.0.2 and ffmpeg 9.0.2, and nothing new is installed there (P24).

The second layer has one heavier run, on the mini PC only, in the desktop session because the share is reachable only there. The engine runs a full file phase on the kept 54 GB Pan's Labyrinth all-tracks rip on `lazymkv-staging` [DEV-3]: the stand-in `makemkvcon` replays the disc's recorded scan and rip from the evidence clone and copies the kept rip into the job's title folder as its output. Real tools then probe and decide; the owner approves the `commentary_detected` hold unchanged (P25); and they remux, verify and file the result. Step 10 never runs on this rip: after MakeMKV removed the empty forced stream, the file holds one English PGS stream, so no subtitle is read (K14). The subtitle reading on real data is the second acceptance disc's run and the OCR trial's arm R on the phase 0 evidence files (part 10.9). This exercises the rest of the file phase without a disc, at the cost of one 54 GB copy on the share per run.

### 12.2 Stand-in tools in phase 2

`crates/fake-tools`' scenario format has argument matching, stdout with timing, an exit code and a copied file named by a directory argument. Phase 2 extends it with exactly what its own code reacts to [PIPE-15]:

- **stderr replay**, from a file, interleaved by its own timing;
- **output files named by an argument**: the file after `-o` for `mkvmerge` and `ffmpeg`, besides the existing directory argument;
- **several responses per run**: when the same arguments come again, the next matching response is used, with a counter kept in the scenario folder, so a first attempt can fail and a second succeed;
- **index drift**: rip output whose `3307` or `3028` title numbers differ from the scan's, for the index guard;
- **hang until killed**: after a given line the stand-in sleeps until killed, for the exit wait, the tree kill [PIPE-3] and cancel [ENG-13];
- **exit without a completion message**: the output stops at a given line and the stand-in exits with a given code, for spec 9.2's rule that such a run is a failure;
- **`MSG:1009` at the scan**, for the switch to the fallback profile [PIPE-11]. One scenario prints it on the first scan only and checks that the second scan and the rips name `fallback.mmcp.xml`, whose selection is `+sel:all,-sel:mvcvideo` (K6, L4). Another prints it on both scans and checks that the job fails with `ScanFailed`, class `Unknown`, quoting MakeMKV's rejection, and that nothing is ripped (part 5.4, L5);
- **an `ffmpeg` stand-in**, chosen like the others by its file stem, for step 10a's decode.

Read errors, stalls, share loss and hash failures wait for phase 3, when the watchdogs and failure classes that react to them arrive (spec 16, row 3).

### 12.3 Scenarios

Small hand-made scenarios go under `corpus/scenarios/`, next to the existing `disc-a-scan/`, and CI runs them [DEV-4]. Each is a synthetic disc: a short scan, rip output, `mkvmerge -J` and `ffprobe` answers, and the files they create. Its lookups use TheDiscDB answers from `corpus/thediscdb-api` and TMDb answers in the shapes of `core`'s test support. A scenario holds no value that only the evidence clone holds, so ruling RP21's `clone_values` check keeps passing. Scenarios built from the owner's recordings are generated at test time from `corpus/owner/<slug>/` and never written under `crates/` or `corpus/scenarios/`; their tests skip without the clone, as the golden tests do, and nothing new is published [DEV-4].

### 12.4 Platform tests

Tests of the Windows implementations that need no hardware run on CI's `windows-latest` runner [DEV-2]: the Job Object tree kill of `ProcessGuard`; `KeepAwake`; a Credential Manager round trip under a test name; the pipe's access list and remote-client refusal as written (P6); a push to a silent pipe client, which must not block (K12); the named mutex; and `ShareConnector` against a local folder. They compile only under `cfg(windows)`, while the traits and fakes compile everywhere.

Tests that need the drive or the share carry `#[ignore]` with a reason and run on the mini PC with `--ignored` [DEV-2], with the owner inserting and removing a disc when a test prints its cue. The `Ejector` test runs over SSH, as experiment 7's eject did. The `DriveWatcher` arrival and removal test runs in the desktop session through phase 0's `job.sh` machinery, because part 3.1 leaves open whether a window in an SSH session gets drive notifications (designer's call; no builder call). The real-share `ShareConnector` tests, P7's fallback to the stored credential among them, run in the desktop session too, because SSH cannot reach the share.

### 12.5 Building and running on the mini PC

The owner's Mac has no Rust toolchain. Every cargo command runs on the mini PC through `scripts/box.sh`, one at a time, as in phases 0 and 1; `box.sh` replaces `C:\lazymkv\src` on every sync and builds into `C:\lazymkv\target` [DEV-16]. Built binaries are copied into `C:\lazymkv\run` before they start, and development runs give the engine the data-folder override (part 3.1). Quick stand-in runs use the SSH console, local folders and stand-in tool paths; real-disc runs and anything that touches the share go through the desktop task that `scripts/engine-task.ps1` registers, with `lazymkvctl` reaching the engine from SSH (P5). The owner must be logged in at the console for desktop-session runs, and no build runs while a real disc is ripping, since a build on the J4125 would change the rip's timing. Tests that read the evidence clone use the mirror at `C:\lazymkv\evidence\owner` through the existing junction and `LAZYMKV_EVIDENCE`. Development never writes to the real Movies or Shows folders (part 6.2), and nothing inside a library root is deleted by the engine or a development script (part 6.3).

**Experiment 9 before the plan (P9).** After this design and before the phase 2 plan is drafted, the owner runs phase 0's Task 16 in full, with the test library's Movies folder of P8, `\\unas-pro\media\lazymkv-test-library\Movies`, as its test folder, a form Task 16's path check accepts. The owner runs Jellyfin 12.1 and no Plex server (P36). Task 16's Step 1 therefore answers its Plex questions with "no Plex server" and gives Jellyfin's version; Step 5 asks for the owner's checks on Jellyfin only; Step 6, if the editions question fails, uses the `jellyfin` layout, which spec 8.9 names for an owner without Plex Pass; and Step 7, the optional check of the `[tmdbid-ID]` names that Jellyfin 10.x needs, is skipped, because Jellyfin 12.1 is not a 10.x version. If the lone-file question (Alien) or the versions question (Inception) fails on Jellyfin, work stops and D11 and spec 8.9 are reopened before any plan. If only the editions question fails, the default `edition_style` changes to `jellyfin`, as spec 8.9 says for an owner without Plex Pass, which affects phase 4, since phase 2 files single-cut movies. Spec 8.9's Plex layouts stay designed for other users and untested. Task 16 needs no disc and no engine code. Its script, `scripts/phase0/make-test-library.ps1`, which places five files named as spec 8.9 prescribes, and its run sheet were written on 2026-10-21 (P35). ### 12.6 The pull requests (P12)

Phase 2 has one implementation plan, whose tasks are grouped into pull requests in this order:

1. **Design.** This document and the spec amendments of part 13. Experiment 9 then runs, and the plan is drafted after its result.

## 14. Left for later phases, contested sentences and the designer's calls

### 14.1 Left for later phases

- **Phase 3.** Whole-disc copy mode (spec 6.3) with D18's fallback, and with it `CopyDisc`, `ExtractTitle` and `EmptyCopyDirectory`. The review data model and every review command beyond an unchanged approval (approve with changes, discard, Set identity, Assign titles, Override tracks, Resolve collision, Remember), with the rest of the pipe message `review_action` (P25, R-A8); Discard is the only way, besides the owner's hand, to free a failed job's data (R-A1). The watchdogs: the silent-scan rule, the `PRGV` stall, the read-error rate, the `mkvmerge` stall (part 5.11's check decides its wording), ending a run when the share drops, and "did not exit" as `MediaReadError` [PIPE-3]. Resume beyond running `core::job::resume` on restart. The retention cleanup's deletion when space is short (P4). The `path_collision` hold for a rename that meets a new file, and for a final folder the share says exists but the engine cannot open (part 6.5, L7).
